Sponsor Check

Can this person be a sponsor or godparent?

Five conditions in canon 874 decide who can be a sponsor for Baptism, reception and Confirmation. Answer the questions to check them one at a time.

Who is the proposed sponsor?

Canon law does not bar a spouse or fiancé, but some parishes prefer a sponsor from outside the couple.

Does the sponsor practise the faith?

The law asks for “a life of faith in keeping with the function”. The pastor judges each case.

Is the sponsor under a Church penalty, such as excommunication, that was imposed or declared?

This is rare. Imposed or declared means a formal decree or sentence of Church authority; an automatic penalty that no authority has declared does not count here.

Eligible

This person can be a sponsor

The sponsor meets the conditions of canon 874: chosen for the role, at least 16, a fully initiated Catholic who lives the faith, not under a penalty and not a parent.

Steps

  1. Give the sponsor’s name to the OCIA team. One sponsor is the norm; a catechumen may have one man, one woman or one of each.
  2. If the sponsor belongs to another parish, many parishes ask for a letter from that parish. This is practice, not law.
  3. The sponsor comes to the rites, prays with the person and goes with them to Mass when possible.
  4. At the Easter Vigil the sponsor stands with them for Baptism or reception and Confirmation. The Church recommends the same person for both sacraments.

Sources

Canon law

  • Canon 872: the sponsor helps the person live the Christian life
  • Canon 873: one sponsor, or one of each sex
  • Canon 874 §1: the five conditions for a sponsor
  • Canon 893: a Confirmation sponsor meets the same conditions; the baptismal sponsor is preferred

Catechism

  • CCC 1255: godparents must be firm believers, ready to help
  • CCC 1311: one of the baptismal godparents for Confirmation

Scripture

  • Acts 9:27
  • Ecclesiastes 4:9-10

Not eligible

A parent cannot be the sponsor

Canon law excludes the father and mother of the person being baptized, at any age. Parents keep their own role and can take part in every rite.

Steps

  1. Choose another Catholic who meets the conditions: an aunt, uncle, grandparent, friend or parishioner.
  2. The parents can still come to the rites and sit with the family.

Ask the pastor

Under 16: possible only by exception

The law sets 16 as the minimum age. The diocesan bishop can set another age, and the pastor can grant an exception for a just cause.

Steps

  1. Ask the pastor whether the diocese sets another age or whether an exception is possible.
  2. Otherwise choose a sponsor who is 16 or older.

Not eligible yet

Confirmation and first Communion come first

A sponsor must be a Catholic who has been confirmed and has received the Eucharist.

Steps

  1. Invite this person to prepare for their own Confirmation or first Communion.
  2. Choose another sponsor for this year.

Godparent with a Catholic

An Orthodox Christian can be a godparent alongside a Catholic

Because of the close communion between the Catholic and Orthodox churches, an Eastern Orthodox Christian can be a godparent for a just cause, together with a Catholic godparent, if the person’s Catholic formation is provided for.

Steps

  1. Choose a Catholic who meets the conditions as the main sponsor.
  2. Ask the pastor to add the Orthodox Christian as a second godparent.
  3. This applies to Baptism. For a candidate who is already baptized, only the Catholic sponsor has a canonical role at Confirmation.

Sources

Canon law

  • Canon 874 §2: baptized non-Catholics serve only with a Catholic sponsor

Catechism

  • CCC 838: the Catholic Church and the Orthodox churches

Scripture

  • Acts 9:27
  • Ecclesiastes 4:9-10

Church documents

  • Ecumenical Directory, n. 98b: an Orthodox godparent together with a Catholic godparent

Christian witness

A witness alongside a Catholic sponsor

A baptized member of another church cannot be the sponsor, but can stand as a Christian witness at a Baptism, together with a Catholic sponsor.

Steps

  1. Choose a Catholic who meets the conditions as the sponsor.
  2. For an unbaptized catechumen, this person can be recorded as a Christian witness of the Baptism.
  3. For a candidate who is already baptized, only the Catholic sponsor has a canonical role.

Sources

Canon law

  • Canon 874 §2: only as a witness, and only with a Catholic sponsor

Catechism

  • CCC 1271: baptized Christians are in real, though imperfect, communion

Scripture

  • Acts 9:27
  • Ecclesiastes 4:9-10

Church documents

  • Ecumenical Directory, n. 98a: a witness to the Baptism together with a Catholic godparent

Not eligible

An unbaptized person cannot be a sponsor or witness

A sponsor must be a fully initiated Catholic, and a Christian witness must at least be baptized.

Steps

  1. Choose a Catholic who meets the conditions.
  2. This person is still welcome at the rites and can support the person in other ways.

Ask the pastor

A marriage the Church does not recognize

Many dioceses read “a life of faith in keeping with the function” to include a marriage the Church recognizes. A Catholic who married outside the Church without permission usually cannot be a sponsor until the marriage is regularized.

Steps

  1. Talk with the pastor; the marriage may be convalidated.
  2. For marriage questions, use the Marriage Navigator.
  3. Meanwhile choose another sponsor.

Sources

Canon law

  • Canon 874 §1, 3°: a life of faith in keeping with the function
  • Canon 1117: Catholics marry in the canonical form unless dispensed

Catechism

  • CCC 1650: civil remarriage after divorce

Not eligible now

A sponsor lives the faith they vouch for

The sponsor stands for the Church’s faith and helps the new Christian live it, so the law asks that they practise it.

Steps

  1. Choose a sponsor who practises the faith.
  2. If this person wants to return, confession is the place to begin, and they could be a sponsor in a later year.

Not eligible

A Church penalty prevents it

Someone bound by a canonical penalty that was imposed or declared cannot be a sponsor while it lasts.

Steps

  1. Choose another sponsor.
  2. The person can ask a priest how the penalty can be lifted.
