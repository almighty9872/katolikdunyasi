Confession

Confession takes only a few minutes. You name your sins out loud to a priest, and God removes them. On the evening of the Resurrection, Jesus gave this exact authority to the apostles: “If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven” (John 20:22-23). The priest speaks the words, but God does the forgiving.

Find the confession times first. Most parishes hear confessions once or twice a week, often on Saturday afternoon. The times are on the parish website or in the bulletin. If none of them suit you, call the parish office and ask the priest for an appointment. In Advent and Lent many parishes add extra times.

Examination of conscience

Ten Commandments

Serious sins first. You must confess every serious (mortal) sin you remember. Smaller (venial) sins are good to confess too, but they are not required (CCC 1456-1458).

Temptation is not sin. An unwanted thought or temptation is not a sin; consenting to it is. For a sin to be mortal it must be a serious matter, done with full knowledge and deliberate consent. These questions name what can be wrong; they do not decide how guilty you are (CCC 1857-1859).

You shall have no other gods before me

God comes first. Putting money, success, fame or a person in God’s place breaks this too, as do superstitions like fortune-telling, horoscopes, magic and lucky charms (CCC 2110-2128).

  1. Have I neglected prayer, or remembered God only in hard times?
  2. Have I trusted in fortune-tellers, horoscopes, magic or lucky charms?
  3. Have money, career, being liked or a relationship taken God’s place?
  4. Have I been ashamed of my faith or hidden it?
  5. Have I knowingly rejected a truth the Church teaches?
  6. Have I despaired of God’s mercy, or sinned thinking “he’ll forgive me anyway”?
  7. Have I received Communion knowing I had an unconfessed mortal sin?
  8. Have I treated the Eucharist, the church or blessed objects without reverence?
  9. Have I used a Ouija board or medium, or tried to contact the dead?
  10. Have I believed that all religions are equally true, or neglected to learn my faith?

You shall not take the name of the Lord your God in vain

Reverence for God’s name and for what is holy. Blasphemy, swearing by God’s name needlessly or falsely, breaking a vow and mocking religion go against it (CCC 2142-2159).

  1. Have I used the name of God, Jesus or Mary in anger, or blasphemed?
  2. Have I sworn by God’s name needlessly or falsely?
  3. Have I broken a promise or vow I made to God?
  4. Have I mocked religion, holy things or believers, or laughed along?

Remember to keep holy the Lord’s Day

Give Sunday to God and to rest: taking part in Mass on Sundays and holy days of obligation, avoiding needless work, and making time for family (CCC 2180-2188).

  1. Have I missed Mass on a Sunday or holy day of obligation without a valid reason?
  2. Have I come late to Mass on purpose, or spent it on my phone?
  3. Did I spend Sunday on needless work, shopping and screens instead of family and rest?
  4. Have I kept others (employees or family) from resting on Sunday?
  5. Have I talked during Mass or distracted others?

Honor your father and your mother

Respect and care within the family and toward everyone with responsibility over us, and responsibility toward those in our care (CCC 2197-2233).

  1. Have I been rude, disrespectful or ungrateful to my parents, or neglected to call or visit?
  2. Have I given elderly or sick family members the care they need?
  3. If a parent, have I made time for my children and given them a good example?
  4. Have I treated my spouse with patience and love, or punished them with silence or sulking?
  5. Have I respected teachers, superiors and just laws?
  6. If I have authority at work, have I treated anyone unjustly? If a student, neglected my studies?

You shall not kill

Covers not only murder but everything that harms human life and health: anger, hatred, bullying, abortion, substance abuse, reckless driving and neglecting one’s health (CCC 2258-2317).

  1. Have I shouted at, insulted or humiliated someone in anger?
  2. Have I held on to hatred, a wish for revenge, or refused to forgive?
  3. Have I deliberately excluded or bullied someone (including online)?
  4. Have I harmed my health through excessive alcohol, smoking, drugs or reckless driving?
  5. Have I had an abortion, pushed someone toward one, or encouraged euthanasia?
  6. Have I hit or hurt anyone, or led someone into sin by my bad example?

Suicide is gravely wrong, though fear, deep distress and illness can lessen responsibility. The Church prays for those who have taken their own lives (CCC 2282-2283).

You shall not commit adultery

Sexuality belongs to the lifelong, total gift of love between husband and wife in marriage. Married or single, everyone is called to live chastely according to their state of life (CCC 2337-2359).

  1. Have I watched pornography or sought out explicit content?
  2. Have I had sexual relations outside marriage?
  3. If married, have I been unfaithful to my spouse or treated them as an object?
  4. Have I masturbated?
  5. Have I led others into sin by the way I dress, my jokes or my messages?

Same-sex attraction is not itself a sin; these questions concern acts (CCC 2358). Civil divorce can be permitted when it is the only way to protect yourself, your children or your rights (CCC 2383).

You shall not steal

Respect what belongs to others and their work, including honest work hours, fair dealing, paying debts, caring for the poor and respecting intellectual property (CCC 2401-2449).

  1. Have I taken what belongs to someone else, failed to return something borrowed, or left a debt unpaid?
  2. Have I wasted work time, taken pay without working, or misused employer property?
  3. Have I cheated in buying, selling, taxes, schoolwork, or used pirated software or media?
  4. Have I paid employees fairly and on time, or given/taken a bribe?
  5. Have I neglected my family’s needs through gambling, or neglected to help the poor?

You shall not bear false witness against your neighbor

Respect for the truth. Lying, slander, detraction (needlessly revealing others’ hidden faults), rash judgment and sharing unverified claims all wound truth (CCC 2475-2492).

  1. Have I lied, twisted or exaggerated the truth?
  2. Have I talked behind someone’s back, slandered them, or revealed a secret?
  3. Have I judged someone without hearing them out?
  4. Have I shared things on social media without being sure they were true?
  5. Have I boasted, flattered to gain something, or failed to repair a reputation I damaged?

You shall not covet your neighbor’s spouse or goods

The final two commandments look at the heart: desiring another person’s spouse (ninth) and envying others’ goods or living in greed (tenth) (CCC 2514-2557).

  1. Have I deliberately dwelt on lustful thoughts and fantasies?
  2. Have I looked with desire at someone else’s spouse or partner?
  3. Have I envied others’ homes, cars, jobs or lives, or grown discontented on social media?
  4. Have I been overly attached to money and possessions, or failed to thank God for what I have?
  5. Have I been glad at someone else’s misfortune?

Catholics group the commandments following St. Augustine, combining idols into the first and splitting coveting into ninth and tenth.

Questions and fears

Good to know

Will the priest tell anyone what I say?

Never. The seal of confession is absolute: a priest may never reveal anything he hears there, to anyone, for any reason, not even to save his own life (CCC 1467). Priests like St. John Nepomucene (1393) and St. Mateo Correa Magallanes (1927) died rather than break it.

Will the priest judge me or get angry?

No. The priest acts in the person of Christ, not in his own name; the forgiveness you hear is Christ’s. He has heard it all before, and most priests are glad you found the courage to come.

It’s been years, and I don’t know how to begin

Tell the priest: “Father, it’s been a long time. Can you help me?” He will walk you through it. Confess the serious sins you remember and roughly how often.

There is one sin I’m too ashamed to say

Say it anyway, as plainly as you can. The priest will not be shocked. Holding back a serious sin knowingly means you haven’t handed it over (CCC 1456), and the sin people dread saying is often the one they feel freest from afterward.

What if I forget what to say, or forget a sin?

The priest will help, and you can read your list from paper or your phone. If you honestly forget a sin, it is forgiven with the others; if it was serious, mention it next time.

Screen or face to face?

Whichever you prefer. Both are valid and common. Church law asks every church to offer a screen for anyone who wants one (canon 964).

How long does it take?

Usually a few minutes. If it has been many years or you have a lot to talk through, it is kind to make an appointment rather than use a busy Saturday slot.

What’s the difference between mortal and venial sin?

A sin is mortal when all three are present: grave matter, full knowledge, and deliberate consent (CCC 1857). If any is missing, it is venial. When unsure, ask the priest.

How often should I go?

The Church asks you to confess serious sins at least once a year (CCC 1457), and before receiving Communion if aware of a mortal sin. Many Catholics go monthly.

I keep confessing the same sins. Is that a problem?

No. Most people struggle with the same few things for years. The point is not to arrive perfect; it is to come back to God every time.

Can I go if I’m not baptized, or not Catholic?

Confession is for baptized Catholics. Baptism itself washes away every sin. If you are preparing to become Catholic, you make your first confession before reception. Anyone can always speak with a priest and ask for a blessing.

Step by step

Flow of confession

Confession has the same shape every time. Once you know it, there is nothing left to be afraid of. If you forget a step, the priest will guide you.

1.Before you go: examine your conscience

Sit quietly, in the church or at home. Ask the Holy Spirit to help you see your life honestly. Then think back over what you have done and failed to do since your last confession, using the examination of conscience above. You can write your sins down on paper or on your phone if that helps.

2.Go in

Wait your turn near the confessional. When it is free, go in. You can kneel behind the screen, where the priest cannot see you, or sit facing him; either is fine. The priest may greet you or read a short passage from Scripture.

3.Begin

Make the Sign of the Cross, then say how long it has been since your last confession. If you do not remember, a guess is fine. If it is your first time, say so.

“Bless me, Father, for I have sinned. It has been three months since my last confession.”

4.Say your sins

Say them simply and plainly. For serious sins, say what they were and, if you can, roughly how many times. You do not need to tell the whole story or defend yourself. When you have finished, say so.

“For these and all the sins of my past life, I am sorry.”

5.Listen to the priest

He may give you a word of advice or ask a question. Then he gives you a penance, usually a prayer or a good deed, as your first step back toward God. Remember it, and if you did not hear it, ask him to repeat it.

6.Say you are sorry: the Act of Contrition

The priest asks you to pray an act of contrition. Use your own words, a short prayer, or the traditional one. You may read it from a card or your phone.

“O my God, I am heartily sorry for having offended thee, and I detest all my sins because of thy just punishments, but most of all because they offend thee, my God, who art all good and deserving of all my love. I firmly resolve, with the help of thy grace, to sin no more, and to avoid the near occasions of sin. Amen.”

Short version: “Lord Jesus, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner.” Vatican News has more acts of contrition ↗.

7.Absolution: you are forgiven

The priest extends his hand over you and says the words of absolution. With these words every sin you confessed is forgiven, completely (CCC 1441-1442).

“God, the Father of mercies, through the death and resurrection of his Son has reconciled the world to himself and sent the Holy Spirit among us for the forgiveness of sins; through the ministry of the Church may God give you pardon and peace, and I absolve you from your sins in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit.”

What you say: “Amen.” Make the Sign of the Cross.

8.Go in peace

The priest sends you out, often with “Give thanks to the Lord, for he is good,” to which you answer “His mercy endures for ever.” Or he simply says “Go in peace.” Do your penance as soon as you can, ideally before you leave the church, and thank God for His mercy.
