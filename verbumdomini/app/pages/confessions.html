St. Augustine of Hippo · written about 397 to 400

The Confessions of St. Augustine

The full text, in thirteen books

Vittore Carpaccio · The Vision of Saint Augustine

St. Augustine wrote the Confessions around 397 to 400, soon after he became bishop of Hippo in North Africa. He tells God the story of his life: his childhood, his years away from the faith, his conversion in a garden in Milan in 386, and his baptism. The last four books are about memory, time and the first chapter of Genesis.

This is Edward Bouverie Pusey’s translation of 1838. We changed its old forms (“You” for “Thou”, “has” for “hath”) and kept his sentences. Hard words are underlined: tap one to see what it means. The best-known passages are marked as treasured passages. References give book and chapter: 10.27 is Book X, chapter 27.

The thirteen books

  1. Book IInfancy and BoyhoodAugustine praises God, then looks back at his infancy and his schooldays: the beatings, the games, and his love of Latin stories over Greek lessons.
  2. Book IIThe Stolen PearsAt sixteen, idle at home, he drifts into lust and joins a gang that steals pears only for the thrill of doing wrong. Why did he love the sin itself?
  3. Book IIICarthageA student in Carthage, he loves the theater, reads Cicero’s Hortensius and starts to long for wisdom, then joins the Manichees. His mother Monica weeps for him and is told he will come back.
  4. Book IVTeacher and FriendFor nine years he teaches rhetoric and lives with a woman he has not married. The death of a close friend breaks him, and he writes his first book, on beauty.
  5. Book VFaustus, Rome and MilanThe famous Manichee Faustus disappoints him. He slips away to Rome, then takes a post in Milan, where he goes to hear Bishop Ambrose preach.
  6. Book VIAmbrose and AlypiusMonica follows him to Milan. Ambrose’s sermons clear away his objections to Scripture, his friend Alypius is caught by the games, and a marriage is arranged for him.
  7. Book VIIFinding GodBooks of the Platonists show him that God is spirit and that evil is not a thing but a lack of good. Only in Christ, the humble Word made flesh, does he find the way.
  8. Book VIIITake Up and ReadThe stories of Victorinus and Saint Antony shake him. In a garden in Milan, torn between two wills, he hears a child chanting “Take up and read” and gives himself to Christ.
  9. Book IXBaptism and MonicaHe gives up teaching, is baptized by Ambrose at Easter 387, and shares a glimpse of heaven with his mother at Ostia, days before she dies.
  10. Book XMemoryNow a bishop, he asks not what he was but what he is. He searches the vast halls of memory for God and looks honestly at the temptations he still faces.
  11. Book XITime and EternityOn the first words of Genesis: what was God doing before He made heaven and earth? Augustine asks what time is, and finds it in the stretching of the mind.
  12. Book XIIHeaven and EarthOn “In the beginning God made heaven and earth”: formless matter, the heaven of heavens, and how one verse of Scripture can hold many true meanings.
  13. Book XIIIThe Days of CreationThe six days of Genesis read as a picture of the Church, ending in the rest of the seventh day, which has no evening.

Treasured passages

  • Our heart is restlessThe opening prayer and the best-known line of the book: we were made for God, and nothing else will satisfy us.1.1
  • Make room in my soulAugustine asks God to enlarge his soul, repair it and make it clean, so that God can come in.1.5
  • The pear treeAt sixteen he and his friends steal pears they don’t even want. He asks why he loved doing wrong for its own sake.2.4
  • In love with lovingHe arrives in Carthage as a student, hungry for love without knowing what he is really looking for.3.1
  • Closer than my inmost selfGod was nearer to him than he was to himself, even while he searched everywhere else.3.6
  • Monica’s dreamHis mother dreams she is standing on a wooden rule and is told that where she stands, her son will stand too.3.11
  • The son of these tearsA bishop sends Monica home with words she never forgot: the son of these tears cannot be lost.3.12
  • The death of a friendHis closest friend dies, and everything he looks at speaks to him of death.4.4
  • Half of my soulHe borrows a poet’s words for his friend: they were one soul in two bodies.4.6
  • Man is a great deepGod counts the hairs on our head; our feelings and the beating of our heart are harder to count.4.14
  • Ambrose reading in silenceAugustine watches the bishop of Milan read without moving his lips, something few people did in those days.6.3
  • The food of grown menHe hears God say: you will not change Me into you, as food is changed; you will be changed into Me.7.10
  • Not yetAs a young man he prayed: give me chastity and self-control, but not yet.8.7
  • Continence calls himSelf-control appears to him like a mother, surrounded by the many people who lived it by God’s grace.8.11
  • Why not now?Weeping under a fig tree, he asks how much longer it will be “tomorrow, and tomorrow”.8.12
  • Take up and readA child’s voice, a page of Saint Paul, and the moment of his conversion.8.12
  • At the window in OstiaMonica and Augustine talk about heaven at a window by the sea, and for a moment they reach it.9.10
  • Remember me at the altarDying, Monica asks for one thing only: that her sons remember her at the altar of the Lord.9.11
  • What do I love when I love You?Not the beauty of bodies or sweet music, but a light, a voice and a food for the soul.10.6
  • I asked the earthHe asks the earth, the sea and the sky, and they all answer: we are not your God; He made us.10.6
  • The palaces of memoryMemory is a vast hall full of treasures, and he walks through it looking for God.10.8
  • Late have I loved YouThe most famous prayer of the book: God was within him while he searched outside.10.27
  • Give what You commandHis prayer for grace: give what You command, and command what You will.10.29
  • A problem to myselfIn God’s sight he has become a problem to himself.10.33
  • What is time?If no one asks him, he knows; if he tries to explain it, he does not.11.14
  • My weight is my loveLove carries him toward God, the way weight carries a stone down and fire up.13.9
  • The Sabbath without eveningThe last prayer of the book: for the peace of the seventh day, which has no evening.13.35
