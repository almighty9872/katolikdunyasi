Saint Augustine of Hippo · written about 397 to 400

The Confessions

His life told to God, in thirteen books

Vittore Carpaccio · The Vision of Saint Augustine

Saint Augustine wrote the Confessions soon after he became bishop of Hippo, in North Africa. It is the story of his restless search for truth, from his childhood to his conversion in a garden in Milan and his baptism, all of it spoken as a prayer to God. The last four books turn from his past to his present: memory, time, and the first chapter of Genesis. People have read it for sixteen centuries, and it is still one of the great books of the world.

This is the full text in Edward Bouverie Pusey’s translation of 1838. We have updated its old English (“You” for “Thou”, “has” for “hath”, “to” for “unto”) and kept his sentences. Each book opens in the reader, with the reading tools of our Bible: highlights, bookmarks and notes, listening, and your progress. The numbers are book, chapter and section, as the Confessions are usually cited: 10.27 is Book X, chapter 27.

The thirteen books

  1. Book IInfancy and BoyhoodAugustine praises God, then looks back at his infancy and his schooldays: the beatings, the games, and his love of Latin stories over Greek lessons.
  2. Book IIThe Stolen PearsAt sixteen, idle at home, he drifts into lust and joins a gang that steals pears only for the thrill of doing wrong. Why did he love the sin itself?
  3. Book IIICarthageA student in Carthage, he loves the theater, reads Cicero’s Hortensius and starts to long for wisdom, then joins the Manichees. His mother Monica weeps for him and is told he will come back.
  4. Book IVTeacher and FriendFor nine years he teaches rhetoric and lives with a woman he has not married. The death of a close friend breaks him, and he writes his first book, on beauty.
  5. Book VFaustus, Rome and MilanThe famous Manichee Faustus disappoints him. He slips away to Rome, then takes a post in Milan, where he goes to hear Bishop Ambrose preach.
  6. Book VIAmbrose and AlypiusMonica follows him to Milan. Ambrose’s sermons clear away his objections to Scripture, his friend Alypius is caught by the games, and a marriage is arranged for him.
  7. Book VIIFinding GodBooks of the Platonists show him that God is spirit and that evil is not a thing but a lack of good. Only in Christ, the humble Word made flesh, does he find the way.
  8. Book VIIITake Up and ReadThe stories of Victorinus and Saint Antony shake him. In a garden in Milan, torn between two wills, he hears a child chanting “Take up and read” and gives himself to Christ.
  9. Book IXBaptism and MonicaHe gives up teaching, is baptized by Ambrose at Easter 387, and shares a glimpse of heaven with his mother at Ostia, days before she dies.
  10. Book XMemoryNow a bishop, he asks not what he was but what he is. He searches the vast halls of memory for God and looks honestly at the temptations he still faces.
  11. Book XITime and EternityOn the first words of Genesis: what was God doing before He made heaven and earth? Augustine asks what time is, and finds it in the stretching of the mind.
  12. Book XIIHeaven and EarthOn “In the beginning God made heaven and earth”: formless matter, the heaven of heavens, and how one verse of Scripture can hold many true meanings.
  13. Book XIIIThe Days of CreationThe six days of Genesis read as a picture of the Church, ending in the rest of the seventh day, which has no evening.
