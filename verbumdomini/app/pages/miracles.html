Miracles

Benozzo Gozzoli · The Raising of Lazarus

Apparitions, relics, bleeding hosts, bodies that didn’t decay. None of this is required belief: the Church calls it “private revelation”, which adds nothing to the faith. But each case below was investigated closely, and the Church judged it worthy of belief. Here they are, in date order, with the most striking scientific findings. Open any miracle to read what happened, the evidence and what the Church teaches.

Marian Apparitions

Four of the most famous apparitions in the world, each approved after a long investigation.

Our Lady of Fatima

Tens of thousands saw the sun “dance” on the very day three children had named months before.Fatima, Portugal · 1917

What happened

On 13 May 1917, three shepherd children in Portugal, Lucia (10), Francisco (9) and Jacinta (7), saw a woman of light above a small holm oak. She asked them to come back to the same place on the thirteenth of each month for six months. In July she said that in October she would work a miracle “so that everyone will believe.”

At noon on 13 October, between 30,000 and 100,000 people gathered in pouring rain. The rain stopped, the clouds parted, and a pale, silvery disc like the sun spun in the sky, threw off colors in every direction and plunged three times toward the crowd. Avelino de Almeida, a reporter for the anticlerical newspaper O Século, wrote the next day that the sun had “danced.” The Church approved the apparitions in 1930.

At a glance

Where
the Cova da Iria, a pasture outside Fatima, Portugal
When
six apparitions, 13 May to 13 October 1917
Who saw her
Lucia, Francisco and Jacinta, aged 10, 9 and 7
The sign
the miracle of the sun, seen by 30,000 to 100,000 people

Why it can’t be explained away

  • The children were not lying. They were questioned again and again, separately, by priests, lawyers, doctors and hostile officials. The local administrator arrested them and threatened them with death; they cried, but they didn’t change their story.
  • It wasn’t hallucination. Hallucinations are private and don’t keep a schedule announced in advance. All three children saw the same figure in the same place, on the days they had named months before. Lucia kept looking away because the light dazzled her; an imagined light doesn’t dazzle.
  • It wasn’t the sun. No one can look at the noon sun for more than a few seconds. The crowd looked at the disc comfortably for ten minutes, and not a single case of eye damage was reported.
  • It was seen from far away. People in villages miles away, some of them facing away from the sun, described the same event. Fr. Philippe Dalleur, a priest and scientist, used the shadows in photographs taken that day to calculate that the light source stood lower in the sky than the sun.
  • Soaked clothes dried in minutes. On a cool, damp day, that would have taken hours.
  • Everything had been foretold. Lucia’s words were written down in a priest’s interview notes weeks before, and the event came at exactly the place and day announced.

The skeptics’ objections

  • “It was a mass illusion.” There were unbelievers in the crowd, and people miles away who expected no miracle saw the same thing. No illusion shows tens of thousands of people the same thing, in the same order, from different angles. Even the skeptical writer Scott Alexander, who studied the case at length, wrote that at times during his research he was “genuinely scared and confused.”
  • “Some said they saw nothing.” Among tens of thousands of witnesses there are one or two known exceptions, and they too say they could look at the disc without pain. If the event was real, a few exceptions are no surprise; if it was imagined, it is surprising there were so few.

What it means

Fatima’s message is simple and old: pray, do penance, stop offending God. What is new is the sign that came with it, announced in advance so that no one could say it was chance. Heaven chose three children who could barely read, and a crowd that included the people most eager to laugh at them.

What the Church teaches

The bishop of Leiria approved the apparitions in 1930. Francisco and Jacinta were canonized in 2017. Like every private revelation, Fatima adds nothing to the faith; it calls us back to live the Gospel more fully in our own time.

CCC 67

For your life

  • Mary asked the children to pray the Rosary every day. Could I pray one decade today?
  • What would it take for me to believe a sign, and what do I do with the signs I have already been given?

Our Lady of Zeitoun

For three years, crowds of Christians and Muslims saw a figure of light above a Cairo church.Cairo, Egypt · 1968-1971

What happened

On the night of 2 April 1968, a figure of light appeared on the dome of a Coptic church in the Zeitoun district of Cairo. The first to see it were two Muslim bus mechanics, who thought a woman was about to jump and shouted for help.

The apparitions went on for three years. Close to a million people, including Christians, Muslims and foreign journalists, saw a glowing figure and dove-shaped lights above the dome. She never spoke and gave no message.

At a glance

Where
the Coptic church of St. Mary in Zeitoun, Cairo
When
April 1968 to 1971
Who saw her
close to a million people: Christians, Muslims and journalists
The first witnesses
two Muslim bus mechanics

What it means

Zeitoun is the apparition without words. There was no message and no single visionary to doubt; only a silent figure seen night after night by whole crowds, many of them not Christian at all. It speaks the language of the Gospel without speaking: Mary pointing beyond herself, over a church where her Son is worshipped.

What the Church teaches

The Coptic Orthodox Church formally approved the apparitions. They never went through the Catholic Church’s process, but both Churches honor them. The Church teaches that devotion to Mary is part of Christian worship and always leads to her Son.

Our Lady of Guadalupe

Mary came to a poor Aztec convert, spoke his language, and left her image on his cloak.Mexico City, Mexico · 1531

What happened

In December 1531, Juan Diego, a newly baptized Aztec, saw a woman on Tepeyac hill near Mexico City. Speaking in Nahuatl, she asked for a church to be built there.

When the bishop wanted a sign, she sent Juan Diego to gather roses that were blooming on the hill in midwinter. He carried them to the bishop in his cloak (tilma); when he opened it, the roses fell out and the image of Mary, still visible today, appeared on the cloth.

The image played a major part in the conversion of millions of native people in the years that followed. The basilica in Mexico City is the most visited Marian shrine in the world.

At a glance

Where
Tepeyac hill, near Mexico City
When
December 1531
Who saw her
St. Juan Diego, a newly baptized Aztec
The sign
roses in midwinter, and the image on his cloak

What it means

Mary did not appear to the bishop or the conquerors, but to a poor native man, in his own language, looking like one of his own people. The message of Guadalupe is that the Gospel belongs to every nation. Within a few years millions of native people asked for baptism.

What the Church teaches

St. Juan Diego was canonized in 2002. Our Lady of Guadalupe is honored as Patroness of the Americas, and her feast falls on 12 December. The Church teaches that Mary, given to us as our mother at the foot of the Cross, cares for all her children.

CCC 971

Our Lady of Lourdes

“I am the Immaculate Conception”: words a peasant girl could not have invented.Lourdes, France · 1858

What happened

On 11 February 1858, in Lourdes in southern France, Bernadette Soubirous, a poor, sickly fourteen-year-old, saw a young woman dressed in white at the entrance to a grotto. Over eighteen apparitions the woman called her to prayer and penance.

Asked her name, she answered: "I am the Immaculate Conception." A peasant girl with no theological training could hardly have known the phrase, and it weighed heavily in the Church’s decision to take the apparitions seriously.

Millions of pilgrims now visit the spring that appeared where Bernadette pointed. Out of thousands of reported cures, only about seventy have been recognized as miracles, after independent doctors found them medically inexplicable.

At a glance

Where
the grotto of Massabielle, Lourdes, France
When
eighteen apparitions, February to July 1858
Who saw her
St. Bernadette Soubirous, aged fourteen
The fruit
a spring, and about seventy cures recognized as miracles

What it means

Four years before Lourdes, Pope Pius IX had defined the Immaculate Conception: Mary was kept free from sin from the first moment of her life. At Lourdes, Mary used the phrase as her name, to a girl who did not understand it. Lourdes is also a place of the sick, and the greatest healing there is often not of the body.

What the Church teaches

The local bishop recognized the apparitions in 1862, after a four-year inquiry. Each reported cure is examined by an international medical committee before the Church calls it a miracle.

CCC 491, 67

For your life

  • Mary called Bernadette to prayer and penance. What is one concrete act of penance I could offer this week?
  • Who do I know who is sick and needs my prayer or my visit?

Relics and Sacred Objects

The two objects scientists have studied most over the centuries.

The Shroud of Turin

A burial cloth bearing an image that science still cannot explain.Turin, Italy

What happened

The Shroud of Turin is a 4.4-meter linen cloth bearing the front and back image of a crucified man. Tradition holds it to be Jesus' burial cloth. It is documented from the 1350s and has been kept in Turin since 1578.

When it was first photographed in 1898, the negative showed a strikingly clear human face: the image on the cloth behaves like a photographic negative. In 1978 the STURP team of about thirty scientists showed that the image isn't made with paint, pigment or brushstrokes. The color sits only on the outermost surface of the linen fibers, in a layer thinner than a wavelength of light.

When it was analyzed in 1976 with the VP-8, a device built for space photographs, the image produced a consistent three-dimensional relief, which ordinary paintings and photos don't. The bloodstains are real human blood, and there is no image beneath them: the blood reached the cloth before the image formed.

The 1988 radiocarbon test dated the cloth to 1260-1390, but the sample came from a corner repaired after a fire in 1532; in 2005 chemist Raymond Rogers found cotton fibers and dye in the sample that are absent from the rest of the shroud. Researchers at Italy's ENEA could reproduce similar coloring only with extremely powerful ultraviolet laser pulses.

At a glance

What it is
a linen cloth 4.4 meters long with the image of a crucified man
Where
Turin Cathedral, Italy (in Turin since 1578)
First recorded
the 1350s
Studied by
the STURP team of about thirty scientists, 1978

What it means

The Shroud shows in detail what the Gospels tell in a few words: the scourging, the crown of thorns, the nails, the wound in the side. Whatever science one day concludes, it puts the Passion before our eyes, and many who have stood before it say it changed the way they read the Gospel.

What the Church teaches

The Church hasn’t declared the shroud “authentic,” because that is a scientific question, not a matter of faith; but popes visit it with deep reverence. St. John Paul II called it a “mirror of the Gospel.” The honor given to a sacred image passes to the one it shows.

CCC 2132

For your life

  • Spend a few minutes with an image of Christ’s Passion. What does it ask of me?
  • Which of my sufferings could I unite to His?

The Guadalupe Tilma

A cloak of cactus fiber that should have rotted centuries ago still carries Mary’s image.Mexico City, Mexico

What happened

Juan Diego's cloak is a coarse cloth woven from maguey fiber, which normally rots within a few decades. Yet despite being displayed for centuries without glass, the image is still clear today.

In 1979, biophysicist Philip Callahan's infrared study found no underdrawing or layers of paint in the main part of the image, while showing that some details, such as the moon, the angel and the rays, were added later. Engineer José Aste Tonsmann reported seeing tiny reflected figures of people standing before her in Mary's pupils, as a living eye would reflect them.

The tilma is Mexico's most sacred object, visited by more than twenty million pilgrims a year.

At a glance

What it is
Juan Diego’s cloak, woven from maguey fiber
Where
the Basilica of Our Lady of Guadalupe, Mexico City
Since
1531
Studied by
Philip Callahan, infrared study, 1979

What it means

The tilma is the sign Mary gave to a doubting bishop, and it is still giving it. A poor man’s working cloak became the most visited image of Mary in the world: God likes to use humble things to carry His greatest gifts.

What the Church teaches

The Church does not rest the faith on scientific studies of the image. It honors the tilma as a sacred image, and the honor given to it passes to the one it shows.

CCC 2132, 971

For your life

  • What “ordinary cloth” in my life could God use to show His love to others?
  • Have I ever asked God for a sign? How did I respond when one came?

Eucharistic Miracles

In the Eucharist, bread and wine become Christ’s Body and Blood while looking exactly the same. In these three cases, tradition holds, the change became visible.

The Miracle of Lanciano

The host became human heart muscle; the wine became blood of type AB.Lanciano, Italy · 8th century

What happened

According to tradition, in the 8th century in Lanciano, Italy, a priest who doubted the Eucharist saw the bread turn into flesh and the wine into blood as he spoke the words of consecration. The flesh and the blood, in five clots, are still on display.

In 1970, with the archbishop’s permission, the seals were broken and the pathologist Odoardo Linoli took samples. The flesh proved to be human heart muscle and the blood human blood of type AB. Ruggero Bertelli, an anatomy professor who was not a believer, examined the slides separately and reached the same conclusion. Linoli published his findings in a scientific journal, and the AB result was reproduced at the University of Siena in 1991.

At a glance

Where
the Church of St. Francis, Lanciano, Italy
When
the 8th century, by tradition
What remains
flesh, and blood in five clots
Studied by
Odoardo Linoli, 1970; confirmed at Siena in 1991

Why it can’t be explained away

  • A forger wouldn’t have used a human heart. Back then there was no way to tell human flesh from animal flesh. Cutting a heart from a fresh corpse was hard and risky; animal flesh was cheap and easy.
  • The flesh looks like a cross-section of a heart, yet the tradition never says so. It is a round slice, hollow in the middle: the view a cardiologist sees on an ultrasound today. A forger who had done this on purpose would have made a point of it in the story.
  • There is no preservative. Linoli found no trace of salt, resin or alcohol. A forger would certainly have salted or treated the flesh, or it would have rotted within days.
  • Blood clots normally break down and putrefy within a day or two. Those at Lanciano sat for centuries in vessels open to the air, yet their proteins kept proportions like those of fresh blood.
  • The records are old. In the 11th century Guitmund, Archbishop of Aversa, tells of a miracle in Italy in which bread became flesh and wine became blood, and the relics were sealed in the altar. The bishop’s inspection of 1574 is recorded on a marble inscription in the church.

The skeptics’ objections

  • “The test can’t tell human from animal.” Linoli used a forensic test that courts relied on to determine species. The reaction was strong and came within minutes; the cattle and rabbit controls were completely negative.
  • “Bacteria produced the AB result.” Linoli found no bacterial colonies where the sample was taken. And studies show that bacteria very rarely produce a false AB result.
  • “The flesh might simply have dried out and kept.” Heart muscle is watery and spoils fast; flesh left to dry in the open rots before its inside dries. And clotted blood can’t be preserved by drying.

What it means

Every Mass, the bread and wine truly become the Body and Blood of Christ, while still looking like bread and wine. At Lanciano, tradition holds, God let a doubting priest see with his eyes what faith sees every day. The miracle does not change what happens at Mass; it shows it.

What the Church teaches

The Church teaches that in the Eucharist Christ is present “truly, really and substantially”: His Body and Blood, soul and divinity. This change is called transubstantiation.

CCC 1374, 1376

For your life

  • Do I come to Communion as if Christ were truly there?
  • Like the priest of Lanciano, where do I doubt? Could I bring that doubt to Jesus in prayer?

The Miracle of Bolsena-Orvieto

Blood on an altar cloth gave the whole Church the feast of Corpus Christi.Bolsena / Orvieto, Italy · 1263

What happened

In 1263 a Bohemian priest who doubted the Eucharist was saying Mass in Bolsena when blood seeped from the host he broke onto the altar cloth.

Pope Urban IV had the event investigated and the cloth brought to Orvieto. The next year he established the feast of Corpus Christi for the whole Church and asked St. Thomas Aquinas to write its prayers. The cloth is kept in Orvieto Cathedral and carried in procession every year on the feast.

At a glance

Where
Bolsena; the cloth is kept in Orvieto Cathedral
When
1263
What remains
the bloodstained altar cloth
What came of it
the feast of Corpus Christi, from 1264

What it means

A single priest’s doubt became the occasion for a feast the whole Church still keeps. Every Corpus Christi procession through the streets goes back to Bolsena: the Lord who is hidden in the host is carried out to meet His people.

What the Church teaches

The Church honors Christ in the Eucharist with worship due to God alone, inside Mass and outside it, in adoration and processions.

CCC 1378, 1376

The Buenos Aires Miracle

A discarded host turned into human heart tissue, examined by a scientist who never knew its source.Buenos Aires, Argentina · 1996

What happened

In 1996, in a Buenos Aires church, a consecrated host that had been found discarded was placed in water to dissolve. A few days later red, blood-like spots appeared on it and kept growing.

The archbishop at the time, Jorge Mario Bergoglio, the future Pope Francis, had it studied scientifically. In 1999 in New York, cardiologist Frederick Zugibe, who wasn't told where the sample came from, reported that it was human heart muscle from the left ventricle of an inflamed heart; the blood type was AB, as at Lanciano.

At a glance

Where
a parish church in Buenos Aires, Argentina
When
1996
Studied by
cardiologist Frederick Zugibe, New York, 1999
Finding
heart muscle from the left ventricle; blood type AB

What it means

The host had been thrown away, and the tissue it became was from a heart under great strain. It is hard not to see in it the heart of Christ, wounded by indifference and still offering Himself.

What the Church teaches

The Church teaches that Christ is present whole and entire in each consecrated host, and remains present as long as the appearances of bread last; that is why a host is never thrown away.

CCC 1377, 1376

For your life

  • Where have I treated holy things carelessly?
  • How can I make an act of reparation for the indifference shown to the Eucharist?

Incorrupt Saints

Some saints’ bodies have been found far less decayed than they should be, years or even centuries after death. Bodies on display often have a thin wax or silicone mask over the face to protect them.

St. Bernadette of Lourdes

The girl who saw Mary at Lourdes, her body spared from decay for decades.Nevers, France

What happened

Bernadette, the visionary of Lourdes, died in 1879 at thirty-five. When her body was exhumed in 1909, thirty years after burial, her skin looked astonishingly natural.

When it was decided in 1925 to display the body permanently, her face and hands were covered with a thin wax mask. Today she lies in a glass reliquary in Nevers; beneath the mask is the body that stayed incorrupt for decades. Bernadette was canonized in 1933.

At a glance

Where
the convent chapel of Saint-Gildard, Nevers, France
Died
1879, aged thirty-five
Exhumed
1909, thirty years after burial
Canonized
1933

What it means

Bernadette never claimed to be holy because of what she saw. She became a nun, suffered long illness and said she was used “like a broom” and then put back in the corner. Her incorrupt body is not proof of sanctity by itself; her humble life is.

What the Church teaches

The Church canonizes saints for their heroic virtue, not for an incorrupt body. It honors their relics, and asks them to pray for us as models and intercessors.

CCC 828, 1674

For your life

  • Bernadette was content to be forgotten. Where do I need to let go of wanting to be noticed?

St. Pio of Pietrelcina (Padre Pio)

A friar bore the wounds of Christ for fifty years, and they closed without a scar.San Giovanni Rotondo, Italy

What happened

On 20 September 1918, while the Italian Capuchin friar Padre Pio was praying after Mass, wounds like those of Christ appeared on his hands, his feet and his side (the stigmata). They bled for fifty years. When he died on 23 September 1968, his doctor and photographs taken that night showed the wounds had closed without a scar.

When his body was exhumed in 2008, the hands and feet were well preserved; a silicone mask was made for his face. Millions of pilgrims visit his shrine in San Giovanni Rotondo every year.

At a glance

Where
San Giovanni Rotondo, Italy
The stigmata
20 September 1918 to his death in 1968
Exhumed
2008
Canonized
2002

Why it can’t be explained away

  • The doctors couldn’t explain it. The surgeons Luigi Romanelli and Giorgio Festa wrote that the wounds neither healed nor became infected; an ordinary wound either closes or festers. The atheist professor of pathology Amico Bignami thought the wounds were kept open with iodine tincture. For eight days they were kept under sealed bandages with no medicine at all; they went on bleeding every day.
  • There was no anemia. Padre Pio ate very little, yet lost blood every day for years, and his blood tests always came back normal.
  • They smelled sweet, not foul. Wounds left open for a long time smell bad. Hundreds of people describe a pleasant fragrance coming from Padre Pio’s wounds and blood. Dr Romanelli at first took it for perfume and thought it unfitting for a friar. Dr Festa, who had no sense of smell, described how the people traveling in his car noticed the fragrance, not knowing he had a bloodstained cloth from the wounds in his case.
  • They closed without a scar. A deep wound always leaves a scar when it heals. Padre Pio’s wounds closed after fifty years, just as he died, and left no mark.
  • Healings. In 1949 a dynamite accident at the friary shattered Giovanni Savino’s face and destroyed his right eye entirely. When the bandages came off ten days later his face had healed and his right eye was there; he saw with it for the rest of his life. In 2000 a young boy, Matteo Colella, whose meningitis had caused nine of his organs to fail, recovered against all expectations while his mother prayed to Padre Pio. His doctors and ten consulted experts found the recovery inexplicable; it is the miracle accepted for Padre Pio’s canonization.

The skeptics’ objections

  • “He kept the wounds open with acid.” A note from 1919 was found in which Padre Pio asked a woman for carbolic acid “for sterilizing.” Carbolic acid was a common disinfectant at the time, and Padre Pio spoke about it openly when questioned. Acid doesn’t explain wounds that stayed the same for fifty years, smelled sweet and closed without a scar; as Dr Festa noted, acid would also have destroyed any fragrance.
  • “The famous psychologist Agostino Gemelli thought him a fraud.” Gemelli never examined Padre Pio; he spoke with him for a few minutes in a corridor. In his own 1920 report he admitted that he had seen him without carrying out any medical examination.

What it means

Padre Pio never wanted the wounds and asked God to take away the visible marks. What he spent his days on was the confessional and the Mass. The stigmata drew the crowds; the sacraments are what he gave them.

What the Church teaches

St. John Paul II canonized Padre Pio in 2002. The Church teaches that Christ invites His disciples to take up their cross and share in His suffering for the salvation of others.

CCC 618, 828

For your life

  • What suffering in my life could I offer to God instead of only enduring it?
  • Padre Pio heard confessions for hours every day. When did I last go to confession?

St. Jean-Marie Vianney (The Curé of Ars)

The parish priest who heard confessions sixteen hours a day, found intact forty-five years after death.Ars, France

What happened

Jean-Marie Vianney, the parish priest of Ars, heard confessions for up to sixteen hours a day, and people flocked to him from all over France. He is now the patron saint of all parish priests.

When his body was exhumed in 1904, forty-five years after his death, it was dried out but intact. His incorrupt heart is displayed in a separate chapel, and his body, its face covered with a wax mask, lies in the basilica at Ars.

What it means

Jean-Marie Vianney almost failed his studies for the priesthood and was sent to a village nobody wanted. He changed it by staying in the confessional. His life says that holiness does not need talent, only a heart given entirely to God.

What the Church teaches

The Church honors the relics of the saints and holds them up as models. St. John Vianney is the patron saint of parish priests.

CCC 828, 1674
