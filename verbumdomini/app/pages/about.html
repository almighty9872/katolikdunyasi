About This Site

What Verbum Domini is, what you’ll find here, and how to use it

What this site is

Verbum Domini (Latin for “the Word of the Lord”) is a free guide to the Catholic faith in plain English. It’s for anyone: the curious, people on their way into the Church, and Catholics who want to know their faith better. Everything is taken from the Bible, the Catechism and the Church’s own documents, and every page says where its sources come from.

There are no ads, no accounts, no paywall and no tracking. One person writes and maintains it, and it keeps growing.

What we offer

The site has five sections, in this order in the menu:

  • Library: the Holy Bible, all 73 books of the Douay-Rheims chapter by chapter, with modern book names, Bishop Challoner’s notes, a short guide to each chapter and a study appendix of apocryphal and Ethiopian books; the whole Catechism (the Compendium, 598 questions and answers); Canon Law in plain words; Free e-Books, the classics of the faith in full, starting with The Confessions of St. Augustine, to read here or download; and a glossary of 877 Catholic terms.
  • Guides: the Order of the Mass step by step, the Roman Missal Guide to how a parish should celebrate it, Confession with the Ten Commandments and an examination of conscience, and how to become Catholic.
  • Prayer: the Rosary with today’s mysteries, prayed bead by bead if you like; Divine Mercy, with the Chaplet, the image “Jesus, I trust in you”, the Hour of Mercy and Divine Mercy Sunday; Deliverance Prayers, six steps from preparation to protection afterwards; and the Church’s common prayers in English and Latin.
  • Answers: the Frequently Asked Questions, the parables of Jesus, Why We’re Catholic, and answers for atheists, Muslims and Jewish friends, each argued from reason and their own sources.
  • History: the history of the Rosary, the history of the Church in 62 paintings, Vatican II Explained (the Council, its sixteen documents and common misunderstandings), The Popes, all 267 from St. Peter to today, each with a profile of his life and acts, a saint for every day of the year, and the miracles the Church has examined.

Finding your way

  • Home: the logo always takes you to the Welcome page. It picks up where you left off in the Bible, the Catechism, The Confessions and today’s Rosary, lists every page on the site under Contents, keeps your Backup, and offers downloads.
  • The menu: on a computer, the sidebar lists every page by section; each section folds away with a click on its heading, and [ hides or shows the whole sidebar. On a tablet the bar at the top, and on a phone the bar at the bottom, has Home, Bible, Today and More; More lists every section.
  • Search: press Ctrl+K (⌘+K on a Mac) or the magnifier. It searches every page, section, Catechism question, FAQ answer, glossary term, saint, parable and prayer. Type a Bible reference such as “John 3:16” and it goes straight there. Accents don’t matter: “Avila” finds Ávila.
  • Today: press T or the date at the top. It shows the liturgical season, the day’s Mass readings, the saint of the day and the feasts ahead. The arrows step to other days.
  • References: a Bible reference anywhere on the site opens the passage in a small window over the page, with the words of Christ in red, so you never lose your place. Its link opens the full chapter in our Bible.
  • Long pages have a list of their sections, beside the text on a computer and at the top of the page on a phone; the section you are reading is marked.
  • Pictures open full size when you tap them, with a short description where we have one.
  • Settings: the gear at the top opens accessibility profiles and settings (larger text, contrast, a dyslexia-friendly font and more), the theme and other display options, and Backup. The page stays visible while the panel is open, so you see each change at once; the arrow beside the close button tucks the panel away. Keyboard shortcuts are listed there when your device has a keyboard.
  • Light or dark: the sun and moon button switches the theme. The site follows your device until you choose.

Reading the Bible

  • Going to a passage: on the Holy Bible page, type a reference (“Psalm 23”, “Matthew 5:3”) or any words. Words search all 37,000 verses, in any order; put a phrase in “quotes” to find it exactly. You can search the whole Bible or just one part of it.
  • Reading tools: text size, font, line and letter spacing, page width, background, and which extras show (verse numbers, words of Christ in red, notes, pictures). Focus mode hides everything but the text.
  • Tap a verse to copy it, share it, or highlight it in one of several colors.
  • Highlights, bookmarks and notes: the bookmark button keeps a chapter, and the notepad lists everything you have marked, ready to jump back to.
  • Listen: reads the chapter aloud with your device’s voices, verse by verse. Choose the voice and the speed, and skip back or forward a verse.
  • Auto-scroll moves the page down slowly as you read. Touching the screen stops it.
  • Extras at the end of a verse: small tags open the Catechism question, the Church Fathers or Challoner’s note on that verse, and a thin line marks a major event.
  • Hard words carry a small ring; tap it for the meaning in everyday English.
  • Keys while reading: the arrow keys turn the chapter, F switches focus mode, L starts listening and S starts auto-scroll.

New to the Douay-Rheims? On the Holy Bible page, “Why the Douay-Rheims reads differently” explains its Psalm numbers, its wording and its extra verses.

Backup

Your progress is kept in your browser: the Bible chapters you have read, where you stopped in each book, your highlights, bookmarks and notes, the section of the Catechism you last read, and your place in today’s Rosary. So are your settings. There’s no account. Backup keeps a copy of all of it. You’ll find it on the Welcome page, under Settings, on the Holy Bible page and in each chapter’s tools; it is the same everywhere.

  • Save to a file: download your progress and settings as a small file. Nothing is sent anywhere.
  • Restore from a file: open that file later, on this device or another, and you pick up where you left off.

Reading offline

The Bible works without a connection. Each book you open is kept on your device the first time it loads, so the next time it opens straight from your device, even on a plane or in a church with no signal. The other pages you visit are kept too, and the last copy is shown when you’re offline.

To have every book ready before you need it, press the download button in the bar at the top of the Holy Bible page. It downloads all 73 books with their notes (about 7 MB), and a ring fills as it goes; tap the small ✕ to stop, and the books saved so far stay. When it is done the button shows a check; tap it again to update the offline copy, in case the text has been corrected since. The same download is in Backup, as Save the whole Bible offline.

The offline copy lives in your browser’s storage for this site. Clearing your browser data removes it, and the books simply download again the next time you open them. Offline reading needs a browser that supports it (all current ones do) and is not available in private windows on some browsers.
