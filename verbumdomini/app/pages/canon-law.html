Canon Law

The Code of Canon Law in plain words: the rules that touch a Catholic’s life.

Master Honoré · Bifolium with the Decretals of Gratian

The Church has its own law, the Code of Canon Law. It holds 1,752 short rules, called canons, in seven books. Most of them deal with courts, dioceses and religious orders. A smaller part reaches into ordinary life: baptism, Sunday Mass, confession, marriage, fasting and funerals. This page explains that part in our own words and gives the canon numbers, so you can check the source.

The Code applies to the Latin (Roman) Church. Eastern Catholic Churches have their own code. Where a canon has changed since 1983, this page follows the current text. For your own case, talk to your pastor. Marriage cases go to your diocese’s tribunal.

Who the law binds

Does Church law apply to non-Catholics?

Purely Church-made laws bind only Catholics. Someone baptized in another church, or not baptized, is not bound by rules like Friday abstinence.

At what age does it start?

At seven, when a child is presumed to have the use of reason. Adulthood in Church law starts at 18.

Your rights and duties

Every baptized Catholic, lay or ordained, shares the same dignity. The Code lists what each of you owes and what each of you can claim.

Can I tell my bishop I disagree?

Yes. You may tell your pastors what you need and want. If you know a subject well, you have a right, sometimes a duty, to give your opinion on the good of the Church. Do it with respect and with care for the faith.

Do I have a right to the sacraments?

Yes. You have a right to receive God’s word and the sacraments from your pastors.

Can anyone push me into marriage or the priesthood?

No. You have a right to choose your state of life freely.

Baptism and godparents

How soon should a baby be baptized?

In the first few weeks. Parents should speak to the pastor soon after the birth, or even before. A baby in danger of death is baptized at once.

How many godparents can a child have?

One or two. With two, one is a man and one is a woman. A baptized Christian from another church can stand as a Christian witness beside a Catholic godparent.

Can I choose any name?

Parents, godparents and the pastor should avoid a name that clashes with the Christian faith. A saint’s name is a common choice, but not a rule.

Confirmation

Do I have to be confirmed?

Yes. Every Catholic is obliged to receive it at the proper time. It makes you a firmer witness to Christ.

At what age?

Around the age of reason, unless your bishops’ conference sets another age. Ages vary from one country to another.

Mass, Sunday and Communion

Do I really have to go every Sunday?

Yes, and on holy days of obligation. Also avoid work and business that keep you from worship, rest and the joy of the Lord’s day. Serious reasons, like illness or caring for a sick child, excuse you.

Which days are holy days?

The Code lists ten, from Christmas to All Saints. Each bishops’ conference may drop some or move them to Sunday. In Canada only Christmas and January 1 remain.

Can I receive Communion twice in a day?

Yes, the second time at a Mass you take part in. In danger of death you can receive again as Viaticum.

What if I have committed a grave sin?

Go to confession before Communion. If you have a grave reason to receive and no chance to confess, make an act of perfect contrition and confess as soon as you can.

Confession

How often must I go?

At least once a year if you have grave sins to confess. Confessing smaller sins is recommended, not required.

Can I pick my priest?

Yes. You may confess to any approved priest, even one of another rite. You may also ask to confess behind a screen.

Will the priest ever tell anyone?

No. The seal is absolute. He may not reveal you in any way, for any reason, and may not even use what he heard in ways that could hurt you.

Anointing of the sick

Is it only for the dying?

No. It is for anyone who begins to be in danger from illness or old age. It can be repeated if the illness returns or gets worse.

What if they can no longer ask?

Give it to the sick who would have asked for it, even implicitly, when they could. If in doubt whether the person is still alive, give it anyway.

Marriage

Marriage has more canons than any other sacrament. At its center is one rule. The free consent of a man and a woman makes the marriage, and no one else can give that consent for them.

What is an annulment?

A Church court decides that a marriage was never valid from the start, for example because consent was forced or one person lacked the capacity. It does not end a valid marriage. Until proven otherwise, every marriage is presumed valid.

Can a Catholic marry someone who isn’t Catholic?

Yes, with permission. For a baptized Christian of another church, the bishop gives permission. For someone not baptized, he gives a dispensation, without which the marriage is invalid. The Catholic promises to stay faithful and to do all they can to raise the children Catholic.

Can a Catholic marriage be dissolved?

A valid sacramental marriage that has been consummated ends only with death. The pope can dissolve one that was never consummated.

Fasting and days of penance

  • Under 14Not bound yet. Children still learn small sacrifices that suit their age.
  • 14 and olderNo meat on Ash Wednesday and Good Friday. On every other Friday, no meat or another penance your bishops allow.
  • 18 to 59Also fast on Ash Wednesday and Good Friday. The duty to fast ends on your 59th birthday.
  • ExcusedThe sick, pregnant or nursing women and people doing heavy physical work need not fast. Your pastor can also dispense you.

What counts as fasting?

The Code leaves the details to the bishops. In practice, it means one full meal and two smaller ones that together do not make a second full meal. Abstinence means no meat.

Do I still give up meat on ordinary Fridays?

Every Friday is a day of penance. The universal law says no meat. Your bishops can let you do another penance instead, such as a work of charity or extra prayer. Canada’s bishops allow this.

Priests, bishops and the pope

How does a man become pope?

The cardinals elect him. He takes up the office when he accepts, if he is already a bishop. If not, he is ordained bishop at once.

Why can’t priests marry?

Latin rite priests promise celibacy, to give their whole heart to Christ and their people. It is a discipline of the Latin Church, not a doctrine. Married men can become permanent deacons.

Can a priest run for office?

No. Clergy may not hold public office that shares in civil power, and may not take an active part in political parties without permission.

Your parish

What does my pastor owe me?

Preaching on Sundays, the sacraments, teaching the faith, knowing his people, visiting the sick and caring for the poor. Every Sunday he offers a Mass for his people.

Can a church charge to get in?

Not during services. Entry to a church is free during sacred celebrations.

Saints, relics, vows and blessings

Can I buy a relic?

No. Selling relics is strictly forbidden. Important relics cannot be given away or moved for good without Rome’s permission.

Who can perform an exorcism?

Only a priest with the bishop’s explicit permission. The bishop gives it only to a priest known for piety, knowledge and prudence.

Funerals and burial

Can ashes be kept at home?

The Code allows cremation. A 2016 Vatican instruction adds that ashes belong in a cemetery or another sacred place, not at home, and are not scattered.

Can a baby who died before baptism have a Catholic funeral?

Yes, if the parents meant to baptize the child, with the bishop’s permission.

Money and the Church

Do I have to pay for a Mass?

No. You may give an offering to have a Mass said for your intention. It supports the Church. The law forbids anything that looks like buying or selling, and urges priests to say Masses for the poor without an offering.

Penalties and excommunication

Church penalties are meant to heal first and punish second. The bishop uses them only when warnings and pastoral care have failed. Book VI was rewritten in 2021; the numbers here follow the current text.

What does excommunication mean?

It cuts a person off from the sacraments and from roles in the Church. It is not a sentence to hell. Its aim is to bring the person back, and it is lifted once they repent.

What is an automatic excommunication?

For a few crimes, the penalty falls the moment the act is done, with no trial. It binds only someone who knew the law and acted freely, and who was at least 18.

Parents, schools and books

Must my children go to a Catholic school?

Parents should choose schools that give a Catholic education. If they can’t, they must make sure their children learn the faith some other way, like parish classes and teaching at home.

The last word

The salvation of souls is always the supreme law in the Church.

Canon 1752

The Code ends with a rule about moving pastors from one parish to another. Its last clause sums up the whole book: every rule above exists to help people reach God. A law that works against that purpose is being misread.

  1. 1752

    When a pastor is transferred, follow the procedure, act with fairness, and keep in mind the salvation of souls, which is always the Church’s highest law.
