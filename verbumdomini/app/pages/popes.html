The Popes

From St. Peter to Leo XIV, all 267.

Perugino · Giving the Keys to Saint Peter

Jesus gave Peter the keys of the kingdom and told him to feed his sheep. Every Bishop of Rome since has inherited that task. Here are all of them, in order: search by name, browse by era, or open any pope to read his life, what he did for the Church, and the documents he published, with links to the official texts on the Vatican’s website.

Apostles and Martyrs

33-311 · 31 popes
  1. 1stPeter33-64The fisherman Jesus called the Rock, chief of the Apostles and first Bishop of Rome.
  2. 2ndLinus67-76The first successor of Peter, probably the Linus greeted by St. Paul.
  3. 3rdAnacletus76-88Third Bishop of Rome, also called Cletus, named in the Roman Canon.
  4. 4thClement I88-97Author of the earliest Christian writing outside the New Testament, the Letter to the Corinthians.
  5. 5thEvaristus97-105Fourth successor of Peter, honored as a martyr.
  6. 6thAlexander I105-115Early Bishop of Rome to whom tradition credits the blessing of holy water.
  7. 7thSixtus I115-125Sixth successor of Peter, honored as a martyr.
  8. 8thTelesphorus125-136The one early pope whose martyrdom is attested by Irenaeus.
  9. 9thHyginus136-140Pope when the Gnostic teachers Valentinus and Cerdo came to Rome.
  10. 10thPius I140-155Brother of the author of the Shepherd of Hermas; faced the heretic Marcion.
  11. 11thAnicetus155-166Received St. Polycarp in Rome and kept peace despite their different Easter dates.
  12. 12thSoter166-175Known for the generous charity of the Roman Church toward other churches.
  13. 13thEleutherius175-189Pope when the Montanist movement and the martyrs of Lyon reached Rome.
  14. 14thVictor I189-199The first Latin-speaking pope from Africa; insisted on Easter on Sunday.
  15. 15thZephyrinus199-217Led the Roman Church through debates on the Trinity, with Callixtus as his deacon.
  16. 16thCallixtus I217-222A former slave who became pope and defended mercy toward repentant sinners.
  17. 17thUrban I222-230Governed the Church in a time of relative peace under Emperor Alexander Severus.
  18. 18thPontian230-235The first pope known to have resigned, exiled to the mines of Sardinia.
  19. 19thAnterus235-236Reigned only about six weeks; his tomb was found in the papal crypt.
  20. 20thFabian236-250A layman chosen when a dove settled on his head; martyred under Decius.
  21. 21stCornelius251-253Upheld mercy for Christians who had lapsed under persecution, against Novatian.
  22. 22ndLucius I253-254Exiled soon after his election; continued Cornelius’ mercy toward the lapsed.
  23. 23rdStephen I254-257Defended the validity of baptism given by heretics against Cyprian.
  24. 24thSixtus II257-258Beheaded while preaching in the catacombs, with four of his deacons; St. Lawrence followed days later.
  25. 25thDionysius260-268Rebuilt the Roman Church after Valerian’s persecution and clarified teaching on the Trinity.
  26. 26thFelix I269-274Wrote on the divinity and humanity of Christ.
  27. 27thEutychian275-283Pope in a quiet time before the Great Persecution.
  28. 28thCaius283-296Said by tradition to be a relative of Emperor Diocletian.
  29. 29thMarcellinus296-304Pope during the Great Persecution of Diocletian.
  30. 30thMarcellus I308-309Rebuilt the Roman Church after the persecution; exiled for requiring penance of the lapsed.
  31. 31stEusebius309Exiled to Sicily after a few months over the same dispute about the lapsed.

The Church and the Empire

311-590 · 32 popes
  1. 32ndMiltiades311-314Saw the end of persecution and received the Lateran palace from Constantine.
  2. 33rdSylvester I314-335Pope during the reign of Constantine, the Council of Nicaea and the first great basilicas.
  3. 34thMark336Founded the Basilica of San Marco in Rome.
  4. 35thJulius I337-352Defended St. Athanasius and the faith of Nicaea against the Arians.
  5. 36thLiberius352-366Exiled by an Arian emperor for refusing to condemn Athanasius.
  6. 37thDamasus I366-384Patron of St. Jerome’s Bible, poet of the martyrs, and champion of the Roman See.
  7. 38thSiricius384-399Author of the oldest surviving papal decretal.
  8. 39thAnastasius I399-401Condemned certain teachings attributed to Origen; praised by Jerome.
  9. 40thInnocent I401-417Pope during the sack of Rome by Alaric; strengthened Roman authority in the West.
  10. 41stZosimus417-418Wavered over Pelagius before finally condemning him in his Tractoria.
  11. 42ndBoniface I418-422Overcame the antipope Eulalius and supported St. Augustine against the Pelagians.
  12. 43rdCelestine I422-432Condemned Nestorius and sent his legates to the Council of Ephesus.
  13. 44thSixtus III432-440Built Santa Maria Maggiore to honor Mary as Mother of God after Ephesus.
  14. 45thLeo I440-461“Leo the Great”: his Tome guided the Council of Chalcedon, and he met Attila in 452.
  15. 46thHilarius461-468Legate at the “Robber Synod” of Ephesus who escaped to tell the tale; later a strong pope.
  16. 47thSimplicius468-483Pope when the Western Roman Empire fell in 476.
  17. 48thFelix III483-492Excommunicated Patriarch Acacius, beginning the 35-year Acacian Schism.
  18. 49thGelasius I492-496Set out the “two powers” that govern the world: priestly authority and royal power.
  19. 50thAnastasius II496-498Sought to heal the Acacian Schism; Dante placed him in hell by mistake.
  20. 51stSymmachus498-514Survived the Laurentian Schism; a synod declared that no one may judge the pope.
  21. 52ndHormisdas514-523Ended the Acacian Schism with the Formula of Hormisdas, signed by the Eastern bishops.
  22. 53rdJohn I523-526Sent to Constantinople by Theodoric; died in his prison at Ravenna.
  23. 54thFelix IV526-530Turned Roman buildings in the Forum into the Basilica of Sts. Cosmas and Damian.
  24. 55thBoniface II530-532The first pope of Germanic descent.
  25. 56thJohn II533-535The first pope to take a new name, since his own was that of a pagan god.
  26. 57thAgapetus I535-536Deposed the Monophysite Patriarch Anthimus in Constantinople.
  27. 58thSilverius536-537Son of Pope Hormisdas; deposed by Belisarius at the bidding of Empress Theodora.
  28. 59thVigilius537-555Held in Constantinople for years by Justinian during the Three Chapters dispute.
  29. 60thPelagius I556-561Restored order and relief in Rome after the Gothic War.
  30. 61stJohn III561-574Pope during the Lombard invasion of Italy.
  31. 62ndBenedict I575-579Reigned during the Lombard siege and famine in Rome.
  32. 63rdPelagius II579-590Rebuilt San Lorenzo and sent the deacon Gregory to Constantinople; died of the plague.

The Early Middle Ages

590-1049 · 86 popes
  1. 64thGregory I590-604“Gregory the Great”: monk, pastor, writer, and sender of the mission to England.
  2. 65thSabinian604-606Sold grain from the Church’s stores during a famine, which made him unpopular.
  3. 66thBoniface III607Obtained an imperial decree that Rome was the head of all the churches.
  4. 67thBoniface IV608-615Turned the Pantheon into a church dedicated to Mary and all the martyrs.
  5. 68thAdeodatus I615-618A priest of long service who cared for victims of earthquake and plague.
  6. 69thBoniface V619-625Encouraged the mission in England and confirmed churches as places of sanctuary.
  7. 70thHonorius I625-638Able administrator later condemned for his careless letter on Monothelitism.
  8. 71stSeverinus640Waited almost two years for imperial approval and reigned only two months.
  9. 72ndJohn IV640-642Dalmatian pope who condemned Monothelitism and ransomed captives.
  10. 73rdTheodore I642-649Greek pope from Jerusalem who opposed Monothelitism.
  11. 74thMartin I649-655Condemned Monothelitism at the Lateran Synod and died in exile in Crimea.
  12. 75thEugene I654-657Elected while Martin I was still alive in exile.
  13. 76thVitalian657-672Sent Theodore of Tarsus to Canterbury, who organized the English Church.
  14. 77thAdeodatus II672-676Former monk of St. Erasmus, generous to pilgrims.
  15. 78thDonus676-678Ended the claim of Ravenna to be independent of Rome.
  16. 79thAgatho678-681His letter guided the Third Council of Constantinople, which ended Monothelitism.
  17. 80thLeo II682-683Confirmed the Third Council of Constantinople, including the condemnation of Honorius.
  18. 81stBenedict II684-685Won from the Emperor the right to consecrate popes without waiting for approval.
  19. 82ndJohn V685-686The first of a series of Eastern popes, and the first consecrated without imperial approval.
  20. 83rdConon686-687A compromise candidate who received the Irish missionary St. Kilian.
  21. 84thSergius I687-701Added the Agnus Dei to the Mass and rejected the Quinisext Council.
  22. 85thJohn VI701-705Protected the imperial exarch from mutinous troops and ransomed captives.
  23. 86thJohn VII705-707Son of a Byzantine official, patron of art at Santa Maria Antiqua.
  24. 87thSisinnius708Reigned just three weeks, so crippled by gout he could not feed himself.
  25. 88thConstantine708-715Traveled to Constantinople, the last pope to visit the city until Paul VI in 1967.
  26. 89thGregory II715-731Sent St. Boniface to Germany and resisted the iconoclast Emperor Leo III.
  27. 90thGregory III731-741Condemned iconoclasm; the last pope born outside Europe until Francis.
  28. 91stZachary741-752Approved Pepin as king of the Franks and talked Lombard kings out of war.
  29. 92ndStephen II752-757Crossed the Alps to win Pepin’s help; the Donation of Pepin founded the Papal States.
  30. 93rdPaul I757-767Brother of Stephen II; moved martyrs’ relics from the catacombs into the city.
  31. 94thStephen III768-772Held the Lateran Synod of 769, which barred laymen from becoming pope.
  32. 95thAdrian I772-795Ally of Charlemagne; approved the Second Council of Nicaea on holy images.
  33. 96thLeo III795-816Crowned Charlemagne emperor on Christmas Day 800.
  34. 97thStephen IV816-817Crowned Louis the Pious emperor at Reims.
  35. 98thPaschal I817-824Builder of Santa Prassede and Santa Cecilia, with their brilliant mosaics.
  36. 99thEugene II824-827Accepted the Constitutio Romana, giving the emperor oversight of papal elections.
  37. 100thValentine827Reigned about forty days.
  38. 101stGregory IV827-844Promoted All Saints’ Day on 1 November and fortified Ostia.
  39. 102ndSergius II844-847Pope when Saracen raiders looted St. Peter’s and St. Paul’s in 846.
  40. 103rdLeo IV847-855Built the Leonine Wall around the Vatican and won the naval battle of Ostia.
  41. 104thBenedict III855-858Held firm against the imperially backed antipope Anastasius.
  42. 105thNicholas I858-867“Nicholas the Great”: defended marriage and Church law against kings and bishops.
  43. 106thAdrian II867-872Approved the Slavonic liturgy of Sts. Cyril and Methodius.
  44. 107thJohn VIII872-882Vigorous defender of Rome against the Saracens; the first pope said to have been murdered.
  45. 108thMarinus I882-884The first bishop of another see to become pope.
  46. 109thAdrian III884-885Died on his way to the Diet of Worms; venerated as a saint at Nonantola.
  47. 110thStephen V885-891Fed Rome in famine; forbade the Slavonic liturgy after Methodius’ death.
  48. 111thFormosus891-896Gifted missionary bishop whose corpse was put on trial in the “Cadaver Synod.”
  49. 112thBoniface VI896Elected by a Roman mob; reigned about fifteen days.
  50. 113thStephen VI896-897Held the Cadaver Synod against Formosus; was then imprisoned and strangled.
  51. 114thRomanus897Reigned about three months; probably deposed by the pro-Formosus party.
  52. 115thTheodore II897Reburied Formosus with honor in St. Peter’s during a reign of twenty days.
  53. 116thJohn IX898-900Banned trials of the dead and restored Formosus’ memory.
  54. 117thBenedict IV900-903Crowned Louis the Blind emperor; praised for his charity.
  55. 118thLeo V903Overthrown after about two months by the antipope Christopher.
  56. 119thSergius III904-911Seized the papacy with armed help and opened the era of the Theophylact family’s control.
  57. 120thAnastasius III911-913Reigned under the control of Theophylact’s family.
  58. 121stLando913-914The last pope with a name no earlier pope had used, until Francis in 2013.
  59. 122ndJohn X914-928Led the Christian league that defeated the Saracens at the Garigliano; died in prison.
  60. 123rdLeo VI928Installed by Marozia while John X was still in prison.
  61. 124thStephen VII928-931Another pope of Marozia’s choosing.
  62. 125thJohn XI931-935Son of Marozia, made pope in his early twenties, later held captive by his brother.
  63. 126thLeo VII936-939A Benedictine monk who promoted Cluny’s reform with St. Odo.
  64. 127thStephen VIII939-942Supported the Carolingian king of France against rebel nobles.
  65. 128thMarinus II942-946Reformed clergy and monasteries under Alberic’s protection.
  66. 129thAgapetus II946-955Promoted church reform and missions north of the Alps.
  67. 130thJohn XII955-964Made pope at about eighteen; crowned Otto I and was later deposed by him.
  68. 131stLeo VIII963-965A layman installed by Otto I after the deposition of John XII.
  69. 132ndBenedict V964“The Grammarian,” chosen by the Romans and deposed by Otto I.
  70. 133rdJohn XIII965-972Crowned Otto II and created the Archdiocese of Magdeburg for the mission to the Slavs.
  71. 134thBenedict VI973-974Strangled in Castel Sant’Angelo on the orders of the noble Crescentius.
  72. 135thBenedict VII974-983Brought stability, fought simony and supported monastic reform.
  73. 136thJohn XIV983-984Chancellor of Otto II, starved or poisoned in prison by the antipope Boniface VII.
  74. 137thJohn XV985-996Carried out the first recorded papal canonization, of St. Ulrich of Augsburg.
  75. 138thGregory V996-999The first German pope, cousin of Otto III, whom he crowned emperor.
  76. 139thSylvester II999-1003Gerbert of Aurillac, the most learned man of his age and the first French pope.
  77. 140thJohn XVII1003Reigned about six months under the control of John Crescentius.
  78. 141stJohn XVIII1003-1009Created the Diocese of Bamberg; may have ended his life as a monk.
  79. 142ndSergius IV1009-1012Born Peter “Pig’s Snout”; cared for the poor in famine.
  80. 143rdBenedict VIII1012-1024A layman of the Tusculum family who led armies and worked for reform with Henry II.
  81. 144thJohn XIX1024-1032Brother of Benedict VIII, made pope from layman in a day; crowned Conrad II.
  82. 145thBenedict IX1032-1048The only man to be pope three times (1032-1044, 1045, 1047-1048), and a scandal to the Church.
  83. 146thSylvester III1045Bishop of Sabina, set up against Benedict IX for about seven weeks.
  84. 148thGregory VI1045-1046Paid Benedict IX to resign, meaning well; deposed or resigned at Sutri.
  85. 149thClement II1046-1047Bishop of Bamberg, made pope by Henry III to begin reform; buried in Bamberg.
  86. 151stDamasus II1048Bishop of Brixen, died about three weeks after his consecration.

Reform and Crusades

1049-1305 · 43 popes
  1. 152ndLeo IX1049-1054The pope who launched the Gregorian Reform, traveling across Europe to hold councils.
  2. 153rdVictor II1055-1057Henry III’s adviser and last imperial-appointed reform pope; regent for young Henry IV.
  3. 154thStephen IX1057-1058Abbot of Monte Cassino and determined reformer; made Peter Damian a cardinal.
  4. 155thNicholas II1058-1061His decree of 1059 put papal elections in the hands of the cardinals.
  5. 156thAlexander II1061-1073Reformer from Milan, elected by the cardinals; gave his banner to William of Normandy.
  6. 157thGregory VII1073-1085Hildebrand, the great reformer, who fought lay investiture and humbled Henry IV at Canossa.
  7. 158thVictor III1086-1087Desiderius, the great abbot of Monte Cassino, who accepted the papacy unwillingly.
  8. 159thUrban II1088-1099Called the First Crusade at Clermont in 1095 and organized the papal Curia.
  9. 160thPaschal II1099-1118Approved the Knights Hospitaller; captured by Henry V in the investiture struggle.
  10. 161stGelasius II1118-1119Attacked at his election by the Frangipani; died in exile at Cluny.
  11. 162ndCallixtus II1119-1124Ended the investiture struggle with the Concordat of Worms and held the First Lateran Council.
  12. 163rdHonorius II1124-1130Approved the Premonstratensians and, through the Council of Troyes, the Knights Templar.
  13. 164thInnocent II1130-1143Overcame the schism of Anacletus II with St. Bernard’s help; held the Second Lateran Council.
  14. 165thCelestine II1143-1144A learned pupil of Abelard who lifted the interdict on France.
  15. 166thLucius II1144-1145Died of wounds received while leading an attack on the rebel Roman Senate.
  16. 167thEugene III1145-1153The first Cistercian pope, a disciple of St. Bernard; launched the Second Crusade.
  17. 168thAnastasius IV1153-1154An elderly Roman who made peace in the city.
  18. 169thAdrian IV1154-1159The only English pope.
  19. 170thAlexander III1159-1181Canon lawyer who outlasted Barbarossa’s antipopes and held the Third Lateran Council.
  20. 171stLucius III1181-1185Issued Ad abolendam against heresy, a step toward the medieval Inquisition.
  21. 172ndUrban III1185-1187Clashed with Barbarossa and died, by tradition, on hearing of the defeat at Hattin.
  22. 173rdGregory VIII1187Called the Third Crusade with the bull Audita tremendi during a two-month reign.
  23. 174thClement III1187-1191Made peace with the Roman Commune and returned the papacy to Rome.
  24. 175thCelestine III1191-1198Ordained priest the day before his consecration, at about 85; crowned Henry VI.
  25. 176thInnocent III1198-1216The most powerful medieval pope; approved St. Francis and held the Fourth Lateran Council.
  26. 177thHonorius III1216-1227Approved the Dominicans (1216), the Franciscan Rule (1223) and the Carmelite rule.
  27. 178thGregory IX1227-1241Friend of St. Francis; canonized him, issued the Decretals and created the papal Inquisition.
  28. 179thCelestine IV1241Elected in the first “conclave,” locked in by a Roman senator; died seventeen days later.
  29. 180thInnocent IV1243-1254Great canon lawyer; deposed Frederick II at the First Council of Lyon.
  30. 181stAlexander IV1254-1261Canonized St. Clare and defended the mendicant friars at Paris.
  31. 182ndUrban IV1261-1264A shoemaker’s son who made Corpus Christi a feast for the whole Church.
  32. 183rdClement IV1265-1268A widower and former lawyer to the King of France; patron of Roger Bacon.
  33. 184thGregory X1271-1276Elected after nearly three years; created the conclave rules at the Second Council of Lyon.
  34. 185thInnocent V1276The first Dominican pope, a theologian and colleague of St. Thomas.
  35. 186thAdrian V1276Died before being ordained priest, after suspending the strict conclave rules.
  36. 187thJohn XXI1276-1277The only Portuguese pope, a physician and author of a famous logic textbook.
  37. 188thNicholas III1277-1280First pope to live at the Vatican; Dante placed him among the simoniacs.
  38. 189thMartin IV1281-1285A French pope who backed Charles of Anjou and broke the union with the Greeks.
  39. 190thHonorius IV1285-1287Elderly Roman, crippled by gout, who encouraged the study of oriental languages.
  40. 191stNicholas IV1288-1292The first Franciscan pope; sent the first missionaries to China.
  41. 192ndCelestine V1294The hermit pope who resigned after five months; a saint.
  42. 193rdBoniface VIII1294-1303Proclaimed the first Jubilee (1300) and issued Unam Sanctam; humiliated at Anagni.
  43. 194thBenedict XI1303-1304Dominican Master who stood by Boniface VIII at Anagni; a peacemaker.

Avignon and the Great Schism

1305-1417 · 11 popes
  1. 195thClement V1305-1314The first Avignon pope; suppressed the Knights Templar at the Council of Vienne.
  2. 196thJohn XXII1316-1334Organized the papal administration at Avignon; clashed with the Franciscan Spirituals.
  3. 197thBenedict XII1334-1342A Cistercian reformer who defined the beatific vision and began the Palace of the Popes.
  4. 198thClement VI1342-1352A generous, splendid pope who protected the Jews during the Black Death.
  5. 199thInnocent VI1352-1362A reforming lawyer who sent Cardinal Albornoz to recover the Papal States.
  6. 200thUrban V1362-1370A Benedictine abbot who briefly brought the papacy back to Rome.
  7. 201stGregory XI1370-1378Returned the papacy to Rome in 1377, urged by St. Catherine of Siena.
  8. 202ndUrban VI1378-1389His harsh rule led French cardinals to elect a rival, starting the Great Western Schism.
  9. 203rdBoniface IX1389-1404Roman pope of the Schism who held the Jubilees of 1390 and 1400.
  10. 204thInnocent VII1404-1406Restored the University of Rome during the Schism.
  11. 205thGregory XII1406-1415Resigned at the Council of Constance so the Western Schism could end.

Renaissance and Reformation

1417-1605 · 26 popes
  1. 206thMartin V1417-1431Elected at Constance, ending the Western Schism; rebuilt a ruined Rome.
  2. 207thEugene IV1431-1447Presided over the Council of Florence, which reunited the Greek and Latin Churches for a time.
  3. 208thNicholas V1447-1455The first Renaissance pope; founded the Vatican Library.
  4. 209thCallixtus III1455-1458The first Borgia pope; reopened Joan of Arc’s case, which declared her innocent.
  5. 210thPius II1458-1464Humanist writer who became pope, rebuilt his birthplace as Pienza, and died organizing a crusade.
  6. 211thPaul II1464-1471Built the Palazzo Venezia; quarreled with the humanists of the Roman Academy.
  7. 212thSixtus IV1471-1484Franciscan theologian who built the Sistine Chapel and opened the Vatican Library to scholars.
  8. 213thInnocent VIII1484-1492Openly favored his children; his bull of 1484 encouraged the witch hunts.
  9. 214thAlexander VI1492-1503Rodrigo Borgia: able administrator, notorious for his mistresses and his children.
  10. 215thPius III1503Nephew of Pius II, a reformer who died 26 days after his election.
  11. 216thJulius II1503-1513The “warrior pope” who began the new St. Peter’s and commissioned Michelangelo and Raphael.
  12. 217thLeo X1513-1521Son of Lorenzo the Magnificent; excommunicated Martin Luther.
  13. 218thAdrian VI1522-1523The last non-Italian pope until John Paul II; admitted the Church’s guilt in the crisis.
  14. 219thClement VII1523-1534Pope during the Sack of Rome (1527) and Henry VIII’s break with Rome.
  15. 220thPaul III1534-1549Opened the Council of Trent and approved the Jesuits.
  16. 221stJulius III1550-1555Reopened the Council of Trent; built the Villa Giulia.
  17. 222ndMarcellus II1555Reformer who reigned 22 days; Palestrina’s Missa Papae Marcelli honors him.
  18. 223rdPaul IV1555-1559Severe reformer; co-founder of the Theatines, he created the Roman ghetto and the Index.
  19. 224thPius IV1559-1565Closed the Council of Trent with the help of his nephew St. Charles Borromeo.
  20. 225thPius V1566-1572Dominican saint who carried out Trent: the Catechism, Breviary and Missal; the victory of Lepanto.
  21. 226thGregory XIII1572-1585Gave the world the Gregorian calendar (1582) and founded the Gregorian University.
  22. 227thSixtus V1585-1590Franciscan swineherd’s son who rebuilt Rome and organized the Curia into congregations.
  23. 228thUrban VII1590The shortest reign in papal history: thirteen days.
  24. 229thGregory XIV1590-1591Supported the Catholic League in France against the Protestant Henry of Navarre.
  25. 230thInnocent IX1591A reforming lawyer who reigned two months.
  26. 231stClement VIII1592-1605Reconciled Henry IV of France and published the Clementine Vulgate.

Baroque and Enlightenment

1605-1800 · 19 popes
  1. 232ndLeo XI1605A Medici reformer, disciple of St. Philip Neri, who died after 27 days.
  2. 233rdPaul V1605-1621Completed St. Peter’s façade and nave; warned Galileo in 1616.
  3. 234thGregory XV1621-1623Founded Propaganda Fide and canonized Ignatius, Francis Xavier, Teresa and Philip Neri.
  4. 235thUrban VIII1623-1644Consecrated the new St. Peter’s; patron of Bernini; the pope of Galileo’s trial.
  5. 236thInnocent X1644-1655Condemned Jansenism; built Piazza Navona’s church and fountains.
  6. 237thAlexander VII1655-1667Commissioned Bernini’s colonnade of St. Peter’s Square.
  7. 238thClement IX1667-1669Playwright pope who negotiated the Clementine Peace with the Jansenists.
  8. 239thClement X1670-1676Elected at 79; canonized St. Rose of Lima, the first saint of the Americas.
  9. 240thInnocent XI1676-1689Holy reformer who united Europe to save Vienna from the Turks in 1683.
  10. 241stAlexander VIII1689-1691Condemned Gallicanism and enriched the Vatican Library.
  11. 242ndInnocent XII1691-1700Abolished papal nepotism with the bull Romanum decet Pontificem (1692).
  12. 243rdClement XI1700-1721Condemned Jansenism (Unigenitus) and the Chinese Rites; extended the Immaculate Conception feast.
  13. 244thInnocent XIII1721-1724Last pope from the Conti family; forbade the Jesuits to receive novices in China.
  14. 245thBenedict XIII1724-1730A Dominican who lived as a friar and pastor; poorly served by his ministers.
  15. 246thClement XII1730-1740Blind for much of his reign; built the Trevi Fountain and condemned Freemasonry.
  16. 247thBenedict XIV1740-1758Brilliant scholar and canonist, admired even by Voltaire; issued the first modern encyclicals.
  17. 248thClement XIII1758-1769Defended the Jesuits as Catholic kingdoms expelled them.
  18. 249thClement XIV1769-1774A Franciscan who suppressed the Jesuits under pressure from the Catholic kings.
  19. 250thPius VI1775-1799Faced the French Revolution and died a prisoner of the French at Valence.

Revolution and the Modern World

About this list

The numbering follows the official list of the Holy See, the one printed in the Vatican’s yearbook, the Annuario Pontificio. Leo XIV is the 267th pope. Benedict IX, who held the office three separate times in the eleventh century, is counted at each of his three reigns (145th, 147th and 150th), so 265 men have been pope.

A priest named Stephen was elected in March 752 and died a few days later, before he was consecrated. Older lists counted him as Stephen II, but the official list has left him out since 1961, which is why the later Stephens are sometimes given two numbers, such as Stephen II (III). Antipopes, men who claimed the papacy against a lawful pope, are not included.

For the first centuries the dates are those of tradition: the early lists of Roman bishops give the order and the approximate length of each reign, but rarely exact dates, and much of what later writers said about these popes, especially in the Liber Pontificalis, cannot be checked. Where a story is legend or uncertain, the pope’s page says so. Dates of election are given as recorded at the time (before 1582, in the Julian calendar).

The documents on each pope’s page link to the Holy See’s website, vatican.va, which publishes papal texts mostly from Leo XIII (1878) onward, with a selection from earlier centuries. Some are available only in Latin or Italian; the list says so. Each list is a selection of the most important texts; the Vatican’s site has the rest.
