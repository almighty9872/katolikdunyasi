Texts

  • Catechism: the Compendium of the Catechism of the Catholic Church. The text is © 2005 Libreria Editrice Vaticana; the English is the Vatican’s own.
  • The Holy Bible: the Douay-Rheims Bible in Bishop Challoner’s revision, with Challoner’s notes, both in the public domain; we brought the old spelling and grammar up to date. The appendix and Ethiopian books are public-domain English translations, modernized in the same way; each book names its translation. The words of Christ are marked in red following the public-domain World English Bible. The book introductions, major events, chapter summaries and word meanings were written for this site.
  • The Confessions of St. Augustine: Edward Bouverie Pusey’s translation (Oxford, 1838), in the public domain; we brought its old English up to date and kept his sentences. Book, chapter and section numbers follow the Latin text.
  • Scripture in the popups: the same Douay-Rheims text. The link in the popup opens the passage in the Holy Bible on this site.
  • Today’s readings: the references follow the Lectionary used in Canada; the texts shown are from our Douay-Rheims Bible. The thought of the day is a short excerpt from a Pope’s words, as published in Vatican News’ Word of the Day, with a link to the full text.
  • The Mass: excerpts from the English translation of The Roman Missal © 2010, International Commission on English in the Liturgy Corporation. All rights reserved.
  • The General Instruction of the Roman Missal: the Roman Missal Guide sums up, in our own words, the paragraphs it cites and gives their numbers. The official text is on vatican.va; the Canadian adaptations are in the edition published by the Canadian Conference of Catholic Bishops.
  • Canon Law: the page sums up, in our own words, the canons of the Code of Canon Law (1983, with later changes) and gives their numbers. The English translation on vatican.va is © Canon Law Society of America.
  • The Rosary: the traditional English prayers, in the public domain.
  • Saints: dates follow the General Roman Calendar and the Roman Martyrology.

Written for this site

Everything else (the lives of the saints, the parables, the miracles, the history of the Church, the guides, Answering Islam, Answering Atheism, A Call to Our Jewish Friends) was written for this site from the Church’s documents and reliable sources. Where a page draws on particular sources, they are listed at the end of that page.

  • Answering Islam: hadith numbers follow sunnah.com. The Arabic of the Qur’an comes from the Tanzil project, the English from Pickthall’s translation (1930).
  • Miracles: the sections on Fatima, Lanciano and Padre Pio draw on Ethan Muse’s Motiva Credibilitatis.
  • Divine Mercy: the Chaplet, the image, the Hour of Mercy, the feast and the novena follow the Diary of Saint Maria Faustina Kowalska, Divine Mercy in My Soul; the rest draws on St. John Paul II, Dives in Misericordia (1980) and his homily for the canonization of Saint Faustina (30 April 2000), the Apostolic Penitentiary’s decree on the indulgence for Divine Mercy Sunday (29 June 2002), Pope Francis, Misericordiae Vultus (2015), and the Catechism of the Catholic Church 67, 1471, 1846-1848, 2132 and 2447. The wording of the Chaplet varies slightly between translations. The painting of the image is Eugeniusz Kazimirowski’s Divine Mercy (1934, Divine Mercy Sanctuary, Vilnius), in the public domain; the photograph is from Wikimedia Commons.
  • Roman Missal Guide: a summary, in our own words, of the Canadian bishops’ Pastoral Notes for the Celebration of the Eucharist in Light of the Revised Roman Missal (Concacan Inc., 2012).
