Accessibility

Everyone should be able to read this site comfortably

Our commitment

Verbum Domini is built to work for as many people as possible, including blind visitors using a screen reader. That isn’t a box we ticked once; we keep checking as the site grows.

What’s supported

  • Screen readers: pages have a clear heading order and landmarks (navigation, sections menu, main content, footer). Buttons that show only an icon have a spoken name, popups are announced as dialogs, and changes such as the Today panel’s date and the result of a backup are read out.
  • Keyboard: everything opens and closes with the keyboard alone, and you can always see where the focus is. Ctrl+K (⌘K on a Mac) opens search, T opens Today, and [ hides or shows the sidebar. Escape closes any popup, and while one is open, Tab stays inside it. Pictures open full size with Enter.
  • Reading the Bible: Reading tools let you set the text size (14 to 30 pixels), the font (including Legible, Atkinson Hyperlegible, made for readers with low vision), line and letter spacing, page width, justified or ragged text, and a white, gray or black background. Focus mode hides everything but the text. While reading, the arrow keys turn the chapter, F switches focus mode, L starts listening and S starts auto-scroll.
  • Listening: “Listen” reads a chapter aloud with your device’s voices, verse by verse, marking the verse being read. You can choose the voice and speed, and skip back or forward a verse.
  • Plain words: old or hard words in the Bible carry a small ring; tap it for a short meaning in everyday English. Words of Christ in red, verse numbers, notes, pictures and other extras can each be turned off.
  • Pictures and maps: every picture has a text alternative and opens full size, with a short description where we have one. Each map opens with a short description of what it shows, and the Bible reads complete without them.
  • Touch screens: on phones, panels open as sheets from the bottom. They can be dragged down to close, and each also has a close button, so dragging is never needed.
  • Reduced motion: if “reduce motion” is on in your system settings, the site turns off its non-essential animations.
  • Dark and light themes: pick whichever is easier on your eyes; the site follows your device until you choose, then remembers.
  • Color contrast: text colors aim for the contrast ratios in WCAG 2.2 AA in both themes.
  • Contact form: every field has a visible label, and screen readers announce whether your message went through. Turnstile, the spam check, usually works invisibly and only shows a one-click box when it has to.

Known limitations

One person writes and maintains this site, and it hasn’t had a formal accessibility audit. Everything above was built with WCAG 2.2 AA in mind and checked by hand on desktop and mobile, in both themes. That isn’t a certificate, though, and things slip through. If you find one, please tell us.

Found a problem?

If something doesn’t work with your screen reader, a color is hard to read, or you can’t reach something with the keyboard, write to us through the Contact page. Tell us which page, which device and which assistive technology you were using, and we’ll find it much faster.
