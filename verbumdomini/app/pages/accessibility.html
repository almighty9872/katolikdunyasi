Accessibility

Everyone should be able to read this site comfortably

Our standard

Verbum Domini aims to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at Level AA, the current international standard. It should work for everyone, including people who use a screen reader, a keyboard, voice control or a magnifier.

What’s supported

  • Screen readers: pages have a clear heading order and landmarks (top bar, navigation, main content, side columns, footer). Buttons that show only an icon have a spoken name, popups are announced as dialogs, and changes such as the Today panel’s date and the result of a backup are read out.
  • Keyboard: everything works with the keyboard alone, and you can always see where the focus is. The first Tab on every page offers “Skip to main content”. Ctrl+K (⌘K on a Mac) opens search, T opens Today, ? opens Settings and [ hides or shows the sidebar. Escape closes any popup, and while one is open, Tab stays inside it. Pictures open full size with Enter.
  • One-key shortcuts: shortcuts that use a single letter or symbol can be turned off in Settings, under Keyboard, so they don’t get in the way of voice control or other tools.
  • Accessibility settings: the gear in the top bar opens Settings, with profiles for motor, vision, color and reading needs, and separate switches for higher contrast, less saturated colors, bigger text, wider text spacing, highlighted links, a dyslexia-friendly font, a big cursor and reduced motion. Your choices stay in your browser.
  • Reading the Bible: Reading tools let you set the text size (14 to 36 pixels, twice the usual size; on a touch screen you can also pinch the text to resize it), the font (including Legible, Atkinson Hyperlegible, made for readers with low vision), line and letter spacing, page width, justified or ragged text, and a white, gray or black background. Focus mode hides everything but the text. While reading, the arrow keys turn the chapter, F switches focus mode, L starts listening and S starts auto-scroll.
  • Listening: “Listen” reads a chapter aloud with your device’s voices, verse by verse, marking the verse being read. You can choose the voice and speed, and skip back or forward a verse.
  • Plain words: old or hard words in the Bible carry a small ring; tap it for a short meaning in everyday English. Words of Christ in red, verse numbers, notes, pictures and other extras can each be turned off.
  • Pictures and maps: every picture has a text alternative and opens full size, with a short description where we have one. Each map opens with a short description of what it shows, and the Bible reads complete without them.
  • Touch screens: buttons and links are at least 24 by 24 pixels. On phones, panels open as sheets from the bottom. You can drag them down to close, and each also has a close button, so dragging is never needed. In your list of highlights, bookmarks and notes, swipe a row left to delete it, or use its delete button. In the app saved to your Home Screen, swipe in from the left edge to go back, or use the Back links. On an iPhone or iPad the pages grow with the Larger Text setting (Settings, Accessibility, Display & Text Size).
  • Reduced motion: if “reduce motion” is on in your system settings, or in our Settings, the site turns off its non-essential animations.
  • Dark and light themes: pick whichever is easier on your eyes. The site follows your device until you choose, then remembers.
  • Color contrast: text meets the WCAG 2.2 AA contrast ratios in both themes, and links inside running text are underlined, so color is never the only clue.
  • Contact form: every field has a visible label, and screen readers announce whether your message went through. Turnstile, the spam check, usually works invisibly and only shows a one-click box when it has to.

How we test

In November 2026 we checked every page against WCAG 2.2 AA with an automated tool (axe), in both themes, on a desktop and a phone screen, with the menus and panels open as well as closed, and fixed everything it found. We also test by hand with the keyboard.

Known limitations

One person writes and maintains this site, and it hasn’t had a formal audit by an outside accessibility expert. Automated tools catch only some problems, so some will still slip through.

Found a problem?

If something doesn’t work with your screen reader, a color is hard to read, or you can’t reach something with the keyboard, write to us through the Contact page. Tell us which page, which device and which assistive technology you were using, and we’ll find it much faster.
