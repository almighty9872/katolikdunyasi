Dates follow the date of Easter. The diocese announces where and when the bishop celebrates the Rite of Election.

Catechumens: not baptized

  1. Any time of year

    Inquiry (precatechumenate)

    A time of no fixed length to hear the Gospel, ask questions and begin to pray, with the help of the OCIA team.

    CCC 1229 · Mark 1:15

  2. When the inquirer is ready

    Rite of Acceptance into the Order of Catechumens

    At a Sunday Mass the inquirer asks to become a Christian, is signed with the cross and may be given a book of the Gospels. Their name is written in the book of catechumens.

    Canon 788 §1 · Canon 206 · CCC 1248

  3. Often a year or more

    Period of the catechumenate

    Catechesis, Sunday Mass with the Liturgy of the Word, prayer and works of charity. The length depends on each person and on the norms of the bishops’ conference.

    Canon 788 §2 · Canon 788 §3 · CCC 1247 · CCC 1248

  4. First Sunday of Lent

    Rite of Election

    The bishop, usually at the cathedral, hears the testimony of the godparents and the catechists and chooses the catechumens for the Easter sacraments. From now on they are called the elect.

    CCC 1232 · Luke 4:1-2

  5. Third, Fourth and Fifth Sundays of Lent

    Scrutinies and presentations

    Three scrutinies pray for healing and strength, with the Gospels of the Samaritan woman, the man born blind and Lazarus. The Church presents the Creed and the Lord’s Prayer in the weeks of Lent.

    John 4:5-42 · John 9:1-41 · John 11:1-45

  6. Easter Vigil

    Baptism, Confirmation and first Communion

    The elect are baptized, confirmed and receive the Eucharist for the first time, in one celebration.

    Canon 866 · CCC 1233 · Romans 6:3-4

  7. Easter to Pentecost

    Mystagogy

    The newly baptized, called neophytes, reflect on the sacraments they received and take their place in the parish.

    Acts 2:41-42 · 1 Peter 2:9

Candidates: already baptized

  1. Any time of year

    Inquiry and proof of Baptism

    The parish confirms that the Baptism was valid and gets the certificate. A baptized Christian is never a catechumen.

    Canon 869 §2 · Canon 876 · Baptism Check

  2. Where the parish uses it

    Rite of Welcoming the Candidates

    At a Sunday Mass the candidates are welcomed as people already baptized and marked with the sign of the cross. It is kept distinct from the Rite of Acceptance of catechumens.

    Ecumenical Directory, n. 100 · CCC 1271

  3. As long as each needs

    Formation for full communion

    Catechesis adapted to what the candidate already knows and practises as a Christian. It can be much shorter than the catechumenate.

    Ecumenical Directory, n. 99e

  4. First Sunday of Lent, when they will be received at Easter

    Call to continuing conversion

    Candidates are recognized by the bishop or the pastor, often in the same celebration as the Rite of Election, but they are not elected.

    CCC 1428 · Mark 1:15

  5. Before reception

    First confession

    The rite leaves confession before reception to the candidate’s conscience. Parishes usually encourage it, and it is required for anyone conscious of grave sin.

    CCC 1310 · CCC 1457

  6. Easter Vigil or another Sunday

    Reception into full communion

    The candidate makes the profession of faith, is confirmed by the priest who receives them and receives Communion. Eastern Christians are already confirmed.

    Canon 883 2° · Ecumenical Directory, n. 101 · Ephesians 4:5

  7. Easter to Pentecost and after

    Mystagogy

    The newly received take their place in the life and ministries of the parish.

    Acts 2:41-42

Catechumens and candidates often meet in the same group, but the rites keep them distinct. A baptized Catholic who was never catechized follows the candidates’ path without the reception, since they are already Catholic.

Disclaimer The bishops’ conference and the diocese adapt the timing of the rites, so check the dates with the parish.
