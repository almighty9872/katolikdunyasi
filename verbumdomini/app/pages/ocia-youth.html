Children and Teens

Baptism and the other sacraments for children under 18

Children aged 7 to 17 follow the rules for adults, adapted to their age, but their parents must agree. Younger children are baptized with the rite for infants.

How old is the child?

In canon law a child is presumed to have the use of reason once they have turned 7.

Has the child been baptized?

Do the parents or guardians agree?

A minor stays under the authority of their parents or guardians in most matters.

Infant Baptism

The Order of Baptism of Children, not OCIA

Below the age of 7 a child counts as an infant in canon law. The child is baptized with the rite for children, with the parents’ consent and a founded hope that the child will be raised Catholic.

Steps

  1. At least one parent or guardian asks the parish for Baptism.
  2. The parents and godparents take the parish’s preparation.
  3. The child is baptized, ideally on a Sunday in the parish. Confirmation and first Communion come later, at the ages the diocese sets.

Child catechumen

All three sacraments together

A child who has reached the use of reason is baptized as an adult would be. After a catechumenate adapted to their age, they receive Baptism, Confirmation and first Communion in one celebration.

Steps

  1. A parent or guardian signs the parish’s consent and takes part in the preparation.
  2. The child joins an OCIA group adapted for children, often alongside the parish’s catechesis for their age.
  3. The child celebrates the rites with their group, usually including the Rite of Election in Lent.
  4. At the Easter Vigil, or another date the parish sets, the child receives all three sacraments. Confirmation is not postponed to the usual Confirmation age.
  5. A child of 14 or older may choose to be baptized in the Latin Church or in an Eastern Catholic Church.

Sources

Wait

Without consent, Baptism waits

A minor remains under their parents’ authority. Outside danger of death, the parish accompanies the young person until the parents agree or the young person turns 18.

Steps

  1. The young person can come to Mass, pray and learn the faith.
  2. The pastor can meet the family.
  3. At 18 the young person can ask for Baptism on their own.
  4. In danger of death a person who wants Baptism and accepts the main truths of the faith can be baptized.

Baptized Catholic

Catechesis for first confession, first Communion and Confirmation

A child baptized Catholic is already Catholic and does not enter OCIA. They prepare for the sacraments they have not yet received, in the order and at the ages the diocese sets.

Steps

  1. Bring a recent baptismal certificate to the parish.
  2. Enrol the child in the parish’s sacramental preparation. Older children are often prepared in a group of their own age.
  3. First confession comes before first Communion.
  4. The child is confirmed at the age the bishops’ conference or the diocese sets.

Child candidate

Received into full communion

A child validly baptized in another Christian church is not baptized again. With the parents’ consent, they are received into the Catholic Church, confirmed and given first Communion.

Steps

  1. Bring the baptismal certificate. The Baptism Check shows whether the Baptism is recognized.
  2. A parent or guardian signs the consent.
  3. The child prepares with a group of their age and makes a first confession.
  4. At reception the child makes the profession of faith, is confirmed and receives first Communion. A child from an Orthodox church is already confirmed.
