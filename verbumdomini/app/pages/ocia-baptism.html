Baptism Check

Has this person been baptized, and does the Church recognize it?

Answer the questions to see whether someone enters OCIA as a catechumen, a candidate or a baptized Catholic, with the canons and documents behind each answer.

What is the person’s religious background?

Quakers, the Salvation Army and Christian Science do not baptize with water, so their members count as never baptized.

Which sacraments has this Catholic already received?

A baptismal certificate issued recently by the parish of baptism shows Confirmation and other sacraments in its notes.

Was the baptism given with water and the words “I baptize you in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit”?

Anglican, Lutheran, Presbyterian, Methodist, Baptist and most other mainline churches use water and these words. Immersion and pouring are both valid.

Catechumen

Baptism, Confirmation and first Communion together

Someone who was never baptized becomes a catechumen and receives the three sacraments of initiation in one celebration, normally at the Easter Vigil.

Steps

  1. Join the parish’s OCIA group for the first period of inquiry. It has no fixed length.
  2. Celebrate the Rite of Acceptance into the Order of Catechumens. From then on the Church counts the catechumen as joined to it in a special way, and a catechumen who dies is given a Catholic funeral.
  3. Celebrate the Rite of Election on the First Sunday of Lent, usually with the bishop, and the scrutinies on the Third, Fourth and Fifth Sundays of Lent.
  4. Receive Baptism, Confirmation and first Communion at the Easter Vigil. Baptism forgives all sins, so there is no confession before it.

Sources

Canon law

  • Canon 206: catechumens are joined to the Church in a special way
  • Canon 788: admission to the catechumenate in a liturgical rite after the precatechumenate
  • Canon 851 1°: an adult is admitted to the catechumenate and led through its stages
  • Canon 865 §1: an adult must want Baptism, be instructed and be tested in the catechumenate
  • Canon 866: an adult is confirmed right after Baptism and receives Communion in the same celebration
  • Canon 1183 §1: catechumens are counted among the faithful for funerals

Catechism

  • CCC 1233: adult initiation ends in one celebration of all three sacraments
  • CCC 1247: the place of the catechumenate
  • CCC 1249: catechumens are already joined to the Church
  • CCC 1263: Baptism forgives all sins

Scripture

  • Matthew 28:19
  • John 3:5
  • Acts 2:38

Baptized Catholic

Already Catholic: first confession, Confirmation and first Communion

A person baptized Catholic is already a member of the Catholic Church. Baptism is never repeated and there is no reception into full communion; the person completes their initiation.

Steps

  1. Ask the parish of baptism for a recently issued baptismal certificate.
  2. Prepare with the parish. Many parishes form baptized Catholics alongside the OCIA group, but they are not catechumens and do not take part in the Rite of Election.
  3. Make a first confession before first Communion.
  4. Receive Confirmation and first Communion. A priest needs the bishop’s permission to confirm a baptized Catholic, so the date may be the Easter Vigil or the diocese’s adult Confirmation Mass.

Sources

Canon law

  • Canon 845 §1: Baptism cannot be repeated
  • Canon 884 §1: the bishop confirms, or gives specific priests the faculty to do so
  • Canon 889 §2: a person with the use of reason must be instructed and disposed to be confirmed
  • Canon 890: the faithful are obliged to receive Confirmation
  • Canon 916: someone conscious of grave sin goes to confession before Communion

Catechism

  • CCC 1272: Baptism leaves an indelible mark and is given once
  • CCC 1306: every baptized person not yet confirmed should be confirmed
  • CCC 1310: Confirmation is received in a state of grace
  • CCC 1457: confession of grave sin before Communion

Scripture

  • 1 Corinthians 11:28

Baptized Catholic

Already Catholic: preparation for Confirmation

This Catholic has been baptized and has received Communion, so only Confirmation remains. It is also required before a Catholic marries, when that can be done without grave inconvenience.

Steps

  1. Ask the parish of baptism for a recently issued baptismal certificate with its notations.
  2. Join the parish’s preparation for adult Confirmation.
  3. Go to confession before Confirmation.
  4. Be confirmed by the bishop, or by a priest he has authorized, often at the Easter Vigil or the diocese’s adult Confirmation Mass.

Sources

Canon law

  • Canon 884 §1: the bishop confirms, or authorizes specific priests
  • Canon 889 §2: instruction and disposition for Confirmation
  • Canon 890: the obligation to be confirmed
  • Canon 1065 §1: Catholics are confirmed before marriage if possible

Catechism

  • CCC 1306: every baptized person not yet confirmed should be confirmed
  • CCC 1309: preparation for Confirmation
  • CCC 1310: Confirmation is received in a state of grace

Fully initiated

Nothing more to receive

A Catholic who has received Baptism, Confirmation and the Eucharist is fully initiated. OCIA is not needed, and none of these sacraments is given again.

Steps

  1. A Catholic returning after years away begins with confession.
  2. Ask the parish about formation for adult Catholics if they want to learn the faith again.
  3. A fully initiated Catholic of 16 or older who practises the faith can be a sponsor for someone else.

Sources

Canon law

  • Canon 845 §1: Baptism and Confirmation cannot be repeated
  • Canon 989: grave sins are confessed at least once a year
  • Canon 874 §1: who can be a sponsor

Catechism

  • CCC 1272: Baptism is given once
  • CCC 1457: confession before Communion after grave sin

Scripture

  • Luke 15:20-24

Eastern Christian

Baptism and chrismation are already valid

The Church has no doubt about Baptism in the Eastern churches, and their chrismation is a valid Confirmation, even when the certificate does not mention it. Neither is repeated.

Steps

  1. Bring the certificate of Baptism from the Orthodox or Eastern church.
  2. Talk with the pastor about which Catholic Church the person will belong to. Eastern Christians keep their own tradition and are normally enrolled in the Eastern Catholic Church of the same tradition.
  3. Prepare with the pastor. No more than a profession of the Catholic faith is required, so there is no catechumenate and no Rite of Election.
  4. Make the profession of faith and receive Communion as a Catholic.

Sources

Canon law

  • Canon 845 §1: Baptism and Confirmation cannot be repeated
  • Canon 869: valid baptisms are not repeated, even conditionally

Catechism

  • CCC 838: the Orthodox churches are in deep, though not full, communion with the Catholic Church
  • CCC 1312: in the East the priest confirms at Baptism

Scripture

  • Ephesians 4:5
  • John 17:21

Church documents

  • Ecumenical Directory, n. 99a: Eastern baptisms are valid; chrismation is presumed
  • Orientalium Ecclesiarum 4: Eastern Christians who become Catholic keep their own rite
  • Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches, canon 35: they are enrolled in the Church of the same rite

Candidate

Valid Baptism: received into full communion

A Baptism given with water and the Trinitarian words is valid, so the person is not baptized again. They are a candidate, not a catechumen, and are received into the Catholic Church.

Steps

  1. Get a baptismal certificate or a signed letter from the church of baptism. If there is none, a witness can testify.
  2. Prepare in a way that fits what the person already knows and practises. Candidates are kept distinct from catechumens in the rites.
  3. Make a first confession before reception.
  4. Make the profession of faith, be confirmed and receive first Communion. Confirmation in Protestant and Anglican churches is not recognized, so it is given at reception. This is often at the Easter Vigil, but it can be on another Sunday.

Sources

Canon law

  • Canon 845 §1: Baptism cannot be repeated
  • Canon 869 §2: no conditional baptism without a serious reason to doubt
  • Canon 876: one reliable witness can prove a Baptism
  • Canon 883 2°: the priest who receives someone into full communion can confirm them

Catechism

  • CCC 1271: baptized Christians are already in some communion with the Church
  • CCC 1272: Baptism is given once
  • CCC 1310: Confirmation in a state of grace

Scripture

  • Ephesians 4:5
  • Acts 8:14-17

Church documents

  • Ecumenical Directory, nn. 95, 99: water with the Trinitarian formula is valid; an official certificate is trusted
  • Ecumenical Directory, nn. 100-101: candidates are kept distinct from catechumens and are confirmed before Communion

Catechumen

The words used make the Baptism invalid

Baptism needs both water and the words naming the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. With other words it did not take place, so the person is baptized as a catechumen, not conditionally.

Steps

  1. Check the words with the minister or church that baptized, if possible.
  2. Enter the catechumenate.
  3. Receive Baptism, Confirmation and first Communion at the Easter Vigil.

Sources

Canon law

  • Canon 849: Baptism is valid only with true water and the proper words
  • Canon 864: only someone not yet baptized can be baptized

Catechism

  • CCC 1240: the words of Baptism in the Latin Church

Scripture

  • Matthew 28:19

Church documents

  • Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith (2008): “Creator, Redeemer and Sanctifier” is invalid
  • Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith (2020): “We baptize you” is invalid; those baptized so are baptized again
  • Ecumenical Directory, n. 93: water and the Trinitarian formula

Catechumen

Not recognized as Christian Baptism

The Latter-day Saints and Jehovah’s Witnesses use water and similar words but do not believe in the Trinity, and Oneness Pentecostals baptize in the name of Jesus only. The Church does not recognize these baptisms, so the person enters as a catechumen.

Steps

  1. Explain that this is a judgment about the rite, not about the person’s sincerity.
  2. Enter the catechumenate.
  3. Receive Baptism, Confirmation and first Communion at the Easter Vigil.

Sources

  • Canon 864: only someone not yet baptized can be baptized

Needs inquiry

Find out first, then baptize conditionally if needed

When no one knows whether or how someone was baptized, the parish investigates. If a serious doubt remains, the person is baptized conditionally, in private, so that Baptism is never repeated.

Steps

  1. Ask the church where the Baptism may have happened, and look through family papers.
  2. Ask parents, godparents or others who were present. One reliable witness is enough, or the person’s own sworn word if they were baptized as an adult.
  3. If the Baptism is proven, continue as a candidate; if it is proven that none took place, continue as a catechumen.
  4. If doubt remains, the priest explains conditional Baptism and gives it privately, followed by Confirmation and first Communion.

Sources

Canon law

  • Canon 845 §2: a sacrament in prudent doubt is given conditionally
  • Canon 869: investigation first, then conditional Baptism with an explanation
  • Canon 876: one witness or the oath of someone baptized as an adult proves Baptism

Catechism

  • CCC 1272: Baptism is given once

Scripture

  • Ephesians 4:5

Church documents

  • Ecumenical Directory, n. 99d: conditional Baptism is given in private, after an explanation
